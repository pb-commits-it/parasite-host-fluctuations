## 1. The question

In the project's stochastic ring and network models, time spent in a steady stage (a stage whose exit transfer is not driven by the fluctuating host) alters the loss $D = \ln R_0 - \ln R_0^*$ of the parasite's log reproduction number. At a fixed mean generation time $\mathcal{T}$ and positive host autocorrelation $0 < \phi < 1$, a return-time distribution that is more spread out in convex order has a larger generating transform $R = \mathbb{E}[\phi^L]$ and a smaller second-order loss coefficient (`notes/mechanism-temperature-incubation.md`, §2.4, §3.3, §5.1 Refereed; `../paper/record/section_mech.html`, Statements `C2b`, `C2d`). Conversely, if every stage in the cycle has a fixed integer duration and the environment is ergodic under the full-cycle shift, all timing protection vanishes and $D = -\mathbb{E}\ln h = \sigma^2/2$ for a mean-one lognormal multiplier (Statement `C2f` / `studies.E3-fixed`).

Run R10 (`reports/R10-loss-at-large-noise.md`) computed $D$ for a table of real parasites assuming every stage duration is geometric (memoryless, discrete $\mathrm{CV} = \sqrt{1 - 1/\tau} \approx 1$). Run R10b (`reports/R10b-real-systems-stay-shape.md` and its refereed note) replaced the long steady stage with regular Erlang chains or fixed-plus-geometric stays of the same mean, finding:
- for the two *Schistosoma* rows (`1a`, `1b`), majority protection ($D < \sigma^2/4$) holds across chains $m = 2, 4, 16$, and half the geometric model's protection ($\sigma^2/2 - D$) is lost only inside family-specific brackets $\mathrm{CV} \in [0, 0.043]$ (coarse step) or $[0, 0.102]$ (fine step; $[0, 0.039]$ for fixed+geometric);
- for *Plasmodium falciparum* (`6`), majority protection already fails at a chain of $m = 4$ sub-stages ($\mathrm{CV} = 0.342$ coarse, $0.478$ fine) and half the protection is lost near $\mathrm{CV}_{1/2} \approx 0.19\text{–}0.24$;
- for *Borrelia burgdorferi* (`3a` oak forest, `3b` northern edge), protection is marginal even under geometric stays and fails at $m = 2$ on the coarse step (or has $\mathrm{CV}_{1/2} \approx 0.043\text{–}0.103$ on the fine step);
- for *Echinococcus multilocularis* (`2a`), $D$ exceeds $\sigma^2/4$ even when every stay is geometric ($0.719$ coarse, $0.585$ fine of $\sigma^2/2$).

None of the repository's sources provided a measured spread of individual durations for any stage of any parasite or vector (`reports/R10b-real-systems-stay-shape.md`, line 118). This case study asks three linked questions:
1. **Empirical spread among individuals:** Across open compilations and individual-level experimental or cohort datasets of helminths (trematodes, cestodes, nematodes), malaria parasites (*Plasmodium*), mosquitoes, and ixodid ticks, what is the distribution of the **within-cohort among-individual coefficient of variation** ($\mathrm{CV}_{\text{indiv}}$) by stage functional type and taxon once uncertainty of a mean ($\mathrm{SE}$), replicate-container averaging, censoring, and across-temperature plasticity are separated from individual variation?
2. **Placement against R10b and illustrative life cycles:** When measured stage-duration spreads (and, where individual completion/clearance times are observed, exact empirical distributions) are put through the project's formulas and `studies/engine-R16/lifecycle_engine.py`, where do real stages sit relative to R10b's majority-protection threshold ($D < \sigma^2/4$) and midpoint brackets ($\mathrm{CV}_{1/2}$) for all six R10b real-system rows (plus auxiliary checks on the two 1-host nematode rows `5` and `7`) and for the illustrative life cycles of R16 and R15a?
3. **Robustness vs fragility & CV sufficiency:** Which rows of the real-system table are robust or fragile in light of measured spreads, and does $\mathrm{CV}_{\text{indiv}}$ alone predict the loss within $10\%$ when compared against the full empirical duration law?

## 2. Theoretical predictions, formulas, and engine calls

No new theoretical formula is invented (Rule 3). All model evaluations use existing project formulas and code:

1. **Second-order primitive renewal formula** (`notes/mechanism-temperature-incubation.md` §3.3; Statement `C2b`):
   For a finite primitive discrete-time life cycle at its arithmetic-mean threshold ($\lambda = 1$) with one transfer multiplied by $h_t = \exp(\sigma z_t - \sigma^2/2)$, $z_t$ stationary unit-variance Gaussian AR(1) with lag-1 correlation $0 \le \phi < 1$ and $\theta = \phi/(1-\phi)$, let $L \in \{1, 2, \dots\}$ be the survival-weighted reference return time to that transfer, $\mathcal{T} = \mathbb{E}[L]$, and $R = \mathbb{E}[\phi^L]$ ($\equiv 0$ at $\phi = 0$). Then:
   $$D_2 = \sigma^2 \left[ \frac{1 + 2\theta}{2\mathcal{T}} - \frac{R}{1 - R} \right].$$
   - For a serial chain of $m_k$ equal geometric sub-stages of total mean $\tau_k \ge m_k$ steps (no within-stage death), $\mathrm{CV}_k = \sqrt{1/m_k - 1/\tau_k}$ and $\rho_k = \mathbb{E}[\phi^{L_k}] = (1 + \tau_k/(m_k\theta))^{-m_k}$.
   - For a fixed delay of $\tau_k - g$ integer steps followed by one geometric sub-stage of mean $g \ge 1$ steps, $\mathrm{CV}_k = \sqrt{g(g-1)}/\tau_k$ and $\rho_k = \phi^{\tau_k - g} \frac{\phi/g}{1 - (1 - 1/g)\phi}$.
   - For an empirical discrete duration law $p(\ell) = \mathbb{P}(L_k = \ell)$ on $\ell \ge 1$ (obtained by discretizing uncensored individual durations onto step size $\Delta t$ with mean matched to $\tau_k$ or at native duration), $\rho_k = \sum_{\ell \ge 1} p(\ell)\phi^\ell$.
   - **All-fixed periodic endpoint** (Statement `C2f`): When every stage has deterministic integer duration ($\sum_k \tau_k = n > 1$), the primitive expansion is invalid; under $| \phi | < 1$ (mixing Gaussian AR(1)), cohort ergodicity gives exact $D = \sigma^2/2$ at all $\sigma$.

2. **Finite-noise unexpanded loss $D$ (`lifecycle_engine.py` and `r10b` renewal simulator)**:
   - For chains, memoryless stays, and fixed delays (and multi-mechanism sensitivity switches: contact-limited leaving `E`, alternative hosts `A`, spatial patches `P`, within-stage mortality `M`, lognormal vs bounded two-state host `Driver`), we call `studies/engine-R16/lifecycle_engine.py`:
     `Engine(Description(stages=..., transfers=..., drivers=..., patches=...)).critical().loss(steps=..., burn=..., pairs=..., seed=...)`
     heeding both caveats verified in `verification/engine-R16-check.md`: (i) in `Driver(..., kind='bounded', low=0.05)`, `low` fixes the raw lower level and $\mathrm{Var}(h) = e^{\sigma^2}-1$, whereas R10/R10b use `kind='lognormal'`; we run lognormal for all R10b rows and compare both lognormal and bounded (`low=0.05`) on R16's five illustrative cycles; (ii) `population(..., offspring='shared_groups')` keeps staying independent across individuals, so shared-host outage survival uses the exact R18 single-gap formula $P = 1 - [1 - h(G)\{1 - (1 - p(G))^b\}]^J$ (`notes/exact-limits.md`, line 96) rather than `population(..., 'shared_groups')`.
   - For arbitrary empirical discrete stay kernels $p(\ell)$ (e.g., skewed clonal malaria durations or bimodal/senescent survival laws that are not a single equal-rate Erlang chain), we construct the exact companion renewal survival-progression matrix (age-structured hazard representation $h_\ell = p(\ell)/\sum_{j \ge \ell} p(j)$ for $\ell = 1, \dots, \ell_{\max}$) so it is an exact finite linear life-cycle matrix whose `D2` (resolvent) and unexpanded `D` (common-random-number root) are evaluated identically to all parametric families and verified against known cases first (Rule 7).

3. **Protection metrics (matching R10b exactly)**:
   - Cap (all-fixed lognormal single-transfer bound): $\text{cap} = \sigma^2 / 2$.
   - Majority-protection rule (`verdict` in `checks/r10b_real_systems_stay_shape.py`): `"survives"` if $D < \text{cap}/2 - 3\,\mathrm{SE} = \sigma^2/4 - 3\,\mathrm{SE}$; `"fails"` if $D > \sigma^2/4 + 3\,\mathrm{SE}$; `"undecided"` otherwise.
   - Midpoint protection threshold ($\mathrm{CV}_{1/2}$): where $D = (D_{\text{geometric}} + \sigma^2/2)/2$.

## 4. Estimands and strict individual-vs-mean separation rules

One row in `casestudies/devtimes/results/stage_cv.csv` represents **one cohort / treatment cell**: a single parasite or vector species, a single life stage, and a single constant experimental or cohort condition (fixed temperature, humidity, resource/diet level, host species, and strain). Pooling across temperatures or treatments is forbidden because across-treatment variance measures thermal/environmental plasticity rather than individual stay-duration spread.

### 4.1 Extraction and conversion to $\mathrm{CV}_{\text{indiv}}$
Every extracted cohort is assigned an explicit `dispersion_basis` and `included_primary` (`True`/`False`) flag:
1. **`raw_individual_uncensored`** (`included_primary = True` if $n \ge 10$ and right-censored fraction $\le 0.05$):
   Individual completion, moulting, shedding-duration, clearance, or death times $t_1, \dots, t_n > 0$ are directly observed. Compute sample mean $\bar{t}$, sample SD $s = \sqrt{\frac{1}{n-1}\sum (t_i - \bar{t})^2}$, plug-in $\mathrm{CV} = s/\bar{t}$, and Sokal–Rohlf bias-corrected $\mathrm{CV}_{\text{indiv}} = (1 + \frac{1}{4n})\,s/\bar{t}$. Where check spacing $\Delta_{\text{obs}}$ is known and $\Delta_{\text{obs}}/\bar{t} > 0.05$, also compute Sheppard-debiased $\mathrm{CV}_{\text{sheppard}} = \sqrt{\max(s^2 - \Delta_{\text{obs}}^2/12, 0)}/\bar{t}$ for sensitivity.
2. **`raw_individual_censored`** (`included_primary = True` if uncensored events $d \ge 10$ and right-censored fraction $\le 0.50$; sensitivity check restricts to uncensored only):
   Individual right-censored survival/clearance times $(t_i, \delta_i)$. As shown in pre-analysis Simulation P2b, naive SD of uncensored events underestimates CV by $15\text{–}32\%$ when censoring is $14\text{–}42\%$. Primary estimate uses exact right-censored Weibull maximum likelihood $(\hat{\mu}_{\text{MLE}}, \widehat{\mathrm{CV}}_{\text{MLE}})$, with naive completers-only CV recorded in a separate column for transparency.
3. **`interval_transition_curve`** (e.g., cross-sectional mosquito dissections by day post-infection for *Plasmodium* EIP in `D3`, `D4` where mosquitoes are dissected destructively at $t_1 < t_2 < \dots$):
   Destructive cross-sectional prevalence $P(t)$ is the cumulative distribution function $F_{\text{EIP}}(t) \cdot p_{\max}$ of individual EIP completion (assuming survival independence over the window, as modelled in Stopard et al. 2021). Fit Gamma/Erlang CDF $p_{\max} F_{\text{Gamma}}(t; k, \theta)$ by binomial maximum likelihood on dissected counts $(k_t, N_t)$ per single experiment $\times$ temperature ($d \ge 3$ distinct dissection days spanning the rise from $<20\%$ to $>80\%$ of $p_{\max}$), yielding $\widehat{\mathrm{CV}}_{\text{indiv}} = 1/\sqrt{\hat{k}}$ with profile/Fisher SE. Flagged `cross_sectional_cdf_mle` (`included_primary = True` if total dissected $N \ge 50$ and transition is bracketed; analysed separately in sensitivity).
4. **`reported_SD`** (`included_primary = True` if $n \ge 10$, or if $n$ is unstated in a compilation row where `ErrorUnit` explicitly states individual `SD` — with a mandatory sensitivity check restricting strictly to rows with known $n \ge 10$ and verified individual-level unit):
   $\mathrm{CV} = \mathrm{SD}/\text{mean}$, $\mathrm{CV}_{\text{indiv}} = (1 + \frac{1}{4n})\,\mathrm{SD}/\text{mean}$ (or unadjusted if $n$ is missing, flagged `reported_SD_no_n` and excluded from the strict-$n$ primary subset!).
5. **`reported_SE_with_n`** (`included_primary = True` only when $n \ge 10$ is explicitly individual count, **not** number of replicate vials/cups):
   Convert uncertainty of the mean back to individual spread: $\mathrm{SD}_{\text{indiv}} = \mathrm{SE} \sqrt{n}$, $\mathrm{CV}_{\text{indiv}} = (1 + \frac{1}{4n})\,\mathrm{SE}\sqrt{n}/\text{mean}$.
6. **Excluded / non-identifiable rows** (`included_primary = False`, still logged or summarised by count):
   - `SE_without_n` or `CI_without_n` (uncertainty of a mean without sample size cannot yield individual SD);
   - `replicate_group_SD_or_SE` where $n$ is the number of cages/vials/cups containing multiple individuals of unknown count $m > 1$ (Simulation P2c shows this depresses apparent CV by $1/\sqrt{m}$);
   - `ambiguous_error_unit` (error bar given without stating SD vs SE vs 95% CI);
   - `mean_only` or `range_only` (no SD/SE).

### 4.2 Stage functional categories
Each cohort is mapped pre-analysis into one of five biological stage classes:
1. `S1_endoparasite_vertebrate` — blood-stage or adult parasite infection/patency duration inside a vertebrate host (*Plasmodium falciparum* in humans, adult intestinal nematodes in mammals);
2. `S2_parasite_in_invertebrate_vector_or_snail` — intramolluscan prepatent development, patent cercarial shedding duration in molluscs, and *Plasmodium* extrinsic incubation period (EIP) in mosquitoes;
3. `S3_free_living_parasite_stage` — miracidia, cercariae, and free-living nematode egg/L1–L3 development and survival;
4. `S4_vector_tick_development_moulting` — tick larva/nymph feeding, premoult and moulting durations, mosquito/dipteran juvenile development time, and gonotrophic cycle duration;
5. `S5_vector_tick_adult_or_offhost_survival` — adult mosquito lifespan and off-host tick larval/nymphal/adult survival.

## 5. Pre-registered tests and decision criteria

Every test reports `"supports"`, `"contradicts"`, or `"not decided"` exactly against its criterion below (Rule 4), using 2,000 non-parametric bootstrap resamples over cohorts (seeded at `20261114`).

### Test `H_dev1`: Physiological development/moulting/incubation vs survival/clearance spread
- **Prediction:** Stages governed by sequential development, organogenesis, incubation, or moulting (`dev_like`: prepatent development, EIP, egg-to-L3 development, juvenile vector development, tick premoult/moulting, gonotrophic cycle) are substantially more regular than exponential ($\mathrm{CV}_{\text{indiv}} < 1$, specifically median $< 0.50$), whereas stages governed by mortality, immune clearance, or shedding-host death (`surv_like`: vertebrate infection clearance/patency, patent shedding survival, adult mosquito longevity, off-host tick survival, cercarial survival) have larger relative spread ($\text{median }\mathrm{CV}_{\text{indiv}}(\text{surv\_like}) > \text{median }\mathrm{CV}_{\text{indiv}}(\text{dev\_like})$).
- **Criterion (evaluated on strict primary cohorts with known $n \ge 10$):**
  - `"supports"` if the 95% bootstrap CI upper bound on $\text{median }\mathrm{CV}_{\text{indiv}}(\text{dev\_like})$ is $< 0.50$ **and** the 95% bootstrap CI lower bound on $[\text{median }\mathrm{CV}_{\text{indiv}}(\text{surv\_like}) - \text{median }\mathrm{CV}_{\text{indiv}}(\text{dev\_like})]$ is $> 0$;
  - `"contradicts"` if the 95% bootstrap CI lower bound on $\text{median }\mathrm{CV}_{\text{indiv}}(\text{dev\_like})$ is $\ge 0.80$ **or** the 95% bootstrap CI upper bound on $[\text{median }\mathrm{CV}_{\text{indiv}}(\text{surv\_like}) - \text{median }\mathrm{CV}_{\text{indiv}}(\text{dev\_like})]$ is $< 0$;
  - `"not decided"` otherwise.

### Test `H_sys`: Robustness vs fragility of R10b's real-system rows under measured spreads
For each of the six R10b rows (`1a` *S. haematobium*, `1b` *S. mansoni*, `6` *P. falciparum*, `3a` *B. burgdorferi* oak, `3b` *B. burgdorferi* north, `2a` *E. multilocularis*), evaluate unexpanded $D$ on R10b's fine step (`r = 8`, `11` for *Borrelia*) across the empirical 10th, 25th, 50th, 75th, and 90th percentiles of measured $\mathrm{CV}_{\text{indiv}}$ for the relevant steady-stage biological class (in both the Erlang-chain and fixed+geometric families, plus species-specific empirical duration distributions where measured directly in our datasets):
- **`robust` ("supports" the parking reading under measured spread):** Direct or confamilial/functional individual-level spread data exist ($K \ge 3$ cohorts or $n \ge 30$ individuals for the stage type), $D < \sigma^2/4 - 3\,\mathrm{SE}$ holds across the entire 10th–90th percentile empirical range in both parametric shape families (and empirical kernel if available), and the 10th percentile of measured $\mathrm{CV}_{\text{indiv}}$ lies strictly above R10b's fine-step $\mathrm{CV}_{1/2}$ bracket.
- **`fragile` ("contradicts" sufficiency of R10's geometric-stay assumption for that row):** Direct species or stage-class spread data exist, and within the empirical IQR (25th–75th percentile) of measured $\mathrm{CV}_{\text{indiv}}$ (or under the species' own measured duration kernel), $D > \sigma^2/4 + 3\,\mathrm{SE}$ ("fails" majority protection) **or** measured $\mathrm{CV}_{\text{indiv}}$ enters R10b's $\mathrm{CV}_{1/2}$ bracket.
- **`not decided` (unmeasured directly, or straddling threshold, or no baseline protection to lose):** Either (i) no open dataset measures individual-level duration spread for that species' steady stage directly (so the row can only be bounded by proxy taxa, labelled `bounded_by_proxy`), (ii) $D$ straddles $\sigma^2/4$ across the empirical IQR without passing $\pm 3\,\mathrm{SE}$, or (iii) as in *E. multilocularis* (`2a`), $D > \sigma^2/4$ already holds under all-geometric stays so there was no majority protection to lose.

### Test `H_transform`: Does $\mathrm{CV}_{\text{indiv}}$ alone determine the loss on empirical stage-duration laws?
- **Prediction (Statement `C2d`):** At fixed mean and $0 < \phi < 1$, the loss depends on the full return-time distribution (via $R = \mathbb{E}[\phi^L]$ at second order, and higher cumulants at finite $\sigma$), not on $(\text{mean}, \mathrm{CV})$ alone; moreover, highly right-skewed / overdispersed stays ($\mathrm{CV} > 1$, Statement `C2c`) can reverse the sign or exceed geometric protection.
- **Criterion (evaluated across all uncensored individual cohorts with $n \ge 30$):** Rescale each empirical cohort's individual durations to the fine-step mean stay of R10b's moderate cycle (`6` *P. falciparum*, $L_{\text{mean}} = 60$ fine steps, $F = 8$ fixed steps, $\sigma = 1.40, \phi_{\text{fine}} = 0.75^{1/8}$) and compare $D_2(\text{empirical})$ against $D_2(\text{Erlang chain matched to CV})$ and $D_2(\text{fixed+geometric matched to CV})$ (where $\mathrm{CV} \le 1$; for $\mathrm{CV} > 1$, no serial chain or fixed+geometric law even exists at that CV!).
  - `"supports"` CV-sufficiency (against the need for full distributions) if $\ge 90\%$ of empirical cohorts with $n \ge 30$ are representable by a serial chain ($\mathrm{CV} \le \sqrt{1 - 1/60}$) **and** have $\max(|D_{2,\text{chain}} - D_{2,\text{emp}}|, |D_{2,\text{fg}} - D_{2,\text{emp}}|)/|D_{2,\text{emp}}| \le 0.10$;
  - `"contradicts"` CV-sufficiency (confirming Statement `C2d`/`C2c`'s warning on real biological distributions) if $> 10\%$ of empirical cohorts with $n \ge 30$ either have $\mathrm{CV}_{\text{indiv}} > 1$ (inadmissible for any serial chain of geometric sub-stages) or exhibit $> 10\%$ relative error between matched-CV parametric families and the empirical kernel;
  - `"not decided"` if fewer than 10 uncensored individual cohorts have $n \ge 30$.

## 6. Pre-analysis power and identifiability (computed before seeing any data)

Executed in `casestudies/devtimes/power_identifiability.py` (`results/power_identifiability.txt`, run `2026-11-14 22:03:42 CST` with `data/raw/devtimes/` verified empty):

1. **Single-cohort sample-size power (`P1`, 4,000 Monte Carlo cohorts/cell):**
   - At $n = 20$ individuals, a one-sided test at $\alpha = 0.05$ rejects the exponential baseline ($\mathrm{CV} = 1.0$, false-positive rate `0.052`) with power **`1.000`** for true $\mathrm{CV} \le 0.35$, **`0.984`** at true $\mathrm{CV} = 0.50$, and only **`0.280`** at true $\mathrm{CV} = 0.80$ (`0.565` at $n = 50$).
   - At true $\mathrm{CV} = 0.20$ (near malaria's $\mathrm{CV}_{1/2}$), $n = 20$ certifies $\mathrm{CV} > 0.102$ (above the schistosome fine-step bracket) with probability **`0.938`** (`1.000` at $n \ge 50$). At true $\mathrm{CV} = 0.35$, $n = 50$ simultaneously certifies $\mathrm{CV} > 0.210$ (`0.990`) and $\mathrm{CV} < 0.480$ (`0.869`), whereas $n = 10$ certifies $\mathrm{CV} < 0.480$ only `0.386` of the time.
   - **What cannot be distinguished from no regularity effect:** A single cohort with $n \le 30$ and true $\mathrm{CV} \in [0.75, 1.25]$ cannot reliably distinguish mild senescence or overdispersion from an exponential ($\mathrm{CV} = 1$) stay.

2. **Observational distortions that create false fragility or false regularity (`P2`):**
   - **Interval-censoring floor (`P2a`):** When check spacing is $\Delta_{\text{obs}}/\mu = 0.467$ (e.g., 14-day checks on a 30-day stay), binning inflates true $\mathrm{CV} = 0.10$ to naive `0.197` (and true $\mathrm{CV} = 0.04$ to `0.083`, where Sheppard debiasing collapses to `0.003`). Thus **ultra-low CVs ($\le 0.05$) are unidentifiable unless $\Delta_{\text{obs}}/\mu \le 0.10$**.
   - **Right-censoring truncation (`P2b`):** Truncating an exponential ($\mathrm{CV} = 1.00$) cohort at $T_{\max}/\mu = 1.5$ ($22.6\%$ censored) biases naive completers-only $\widehat{\mathrm{CV}}$ down to **`0.723`** (`0.683` at $T_{\max}/\mu = 1.0$), whereas right-censored Weibull MLE recovers **`0.996`** (`1.004`).
   - **Group-mean / SE conflation (`P2c`):** Mistaking SD across $6$ vials of $10$ individuals for individual SD turns true $\mathrm{CV} = 1.00$ into **`0.310`** (and $\mathrm{CV} = 0.50$ into **`0.156`**). Mistaking SE ($n = 25$) for SD turns $\mathrm{CV} = 1.00$ into **`0.194`**, falsely crossing malaria's $\mathrm{CV}_{1/2}$ threshold; multiplying by $\sqrt{n}(1 + 1/(4n))$ recovers **`0.979`**.

3. **Compilation-level power (`P3a`, 2,000 simulated compilations at $K_{\text{dev}} = 40, K_{\text{surv}} = 25$) and transform spread (`P3b`):**
   - Under mechanistic separation ($\mathrm{CV}_{\text{dev}} \in [0.10, 0.35]$, $\mathrm{CV}_{\text{surv}} \in [0.55, 1.15]$) and moderate separation ($[0.25, 0.50]$ vs $[0.45, 0.85]$), power to call `H_dev1` `"supports"` is **`1.000`** (`0.000` false support under both the all-exponential null and the equal-CV `[0.45, 0.75]` null; `1.000` `"contradicts"` under the all-exponential null; `0.985` `"not decided"` under the equal-CV `[0.45, 0.75]` null).
   - At identical $(\text{mean} = 60, \mathrm{CV} = 0.695)$ on fine-step malaria, Erlang chain (`0.3993`), fixed+geometric (`0.4429`), and right-skewed two-point (`0.5120`) laws differ by **`28.2%`** in $D_2$ (`9.7%` at $\mathrm{CV} = 0.483$), motivating `H_transform`.

## 7. Accounting for confounding and ecological/observational factors

Every process listed in `CASESTUDY_SPEC.md` is classified below by how this study handles it: **(a)** included in the model, **(b)** controlled for in the analysis, **(c)** bounded in sensitivity analysis, or **(d)** cannot be addressed from stage-duration compilations.

| Factor | Handling | How implemented in `analysis.py` / limits |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Mean host density (not only variance) | **(b), (c)** | In linear threshold calibration, mean host density scales $R_0$ (`critical()`), while curved encounter responses $f(H)$ add elasticity $\eta$ and baseline-(i) curvature (`notes/exact-limits.md` H1). Stage-duration CVs are extracted at fixed resource/host treatments (**b**), and host-elasticity scaling ($\eta \in \{0.5, 1.0\}$) is bounded on $D$ (**c**). |
| 2. Seasonality and trend | **(b), (c)** | Laboratory CVs hold abiotic drivers constant (**b**). Field cohorts (`D1` Mali dry-season malaria, `D6` *I. scapularis* enclosures, `D11` mussel shedding) reflect seasonal phenology; we evaluate periodic sinusoidal vs AR(1) forcing in sensitivity (**c**) using `r15a_lib.py` Floquet routines and note calendar-locked tick diapause (`3a`, `3b`). |
| 3. Host memory beyond AR(1) | **(c)** | Vole (`2a`) and oak-forest mouse (`3a`, `3b`) series are oscillatory/AR(2) or mast-driven (`reports/R12-cary-single-population.md` found negative annual $r_1$). Sensitivity sweeps $\phi \in \{0, \phi_{\text{table}}/2, \phi_{\text{table}}, 0.90\}$ (**c**). |
| 4. Temperature and other abiotic drivers of parasite rates | **(b), (a)** | Across-temperature plasticity is strictly separated by computing $\mathrm{CV}_{\text{indiv}}$ *within* each constant temperature/humidity cell (**b**), then testing whether $\mathrm{CV}_{\text{indiv}}$ itself varies with temperature (Spearman $\rho$ within multi-temperature studies) and running a coupled thermal clock-and-completion check (`r15a_lib.py` `DDModel` at measured $m \approx 1/\mathrm{CV}^2$) (**a**). |
| 5. Whether host scarcity wastes or delays the waiting stage | **(a)** | Included directly via `Stage(..., contact=True)` in `lifecycle_engine.py` across R16's illustrative cycles (`E = 0` vs `E = 1` at measured `S` sub-stage counts) (**a**). |
| 6. Shape (spread & higher moments) of stage durations, not only means | **(a)** | Core focus of the study: both the **long steady stage** and the **short fluctuating stage** (`Part B2` of R10b) are evaluated across measured CVs, two parametric shape families (Erlang chain vs fixed+geometric), and exact empirical discrete return-time kernels (**a**). |
| 7. Density dependence in the parasite and in the host | **(b), (d)** | Within-host crowding can shorten or lengthen worm patency/shedding; where infection dose is varied experimentally, cohorts are stratified by dose (**b**). Population-level endemic regulation (`Result 16`) is outside threshold $D$ (**d**). |
| 8. Host feedback (parasite changing host numbers) | **(b), (d)** | Parasite-induced host mortality (`D7`, `D8` infected snails) is part of the realized infectious stay in intermediate hosts (**b**). Endogenous host-population cycling (*T. tenuis* in grouse) is excluded from main-table claims and flagged (**d**). |
| 9. Alternative hosts and spatial spread / immigration | **(a)** | Included in multi-mechanism sensitivity via `lifecycle_engine.py` switches `A = 1` (two independent hosts) and `P = 1` (4 asynchronous patches, $m = 0.15$) combined with measured stay regularity `S` (**a**). |
| 10. Demographic noise at small numbers & shared host fate | **(a), (c)** | Whole-number branching extinction (`Engine.extinction`) on R16's moderate cycle at measured sub-stage $m$, plus R18's exact single-outage survival formula $P(G)$ comparing exponential ($m=1$) vs empirical-$m$ Gamma host/worm lifetimes across outage gaps $G$ (**a**, **c**). |
| 11. Observation and sampling error | **(b), (c)** | Separation of `SD` vs `SE*sqrt(n)` vs group means; Sokal–Rohlf finite-$n$ correction; right-censored Weibull MLE vs naive completers; Sheppard interval-censoring debiasing; and comparison across *ama1* skip rules (0, 1, 2, 3 skips in `D1`) (**b**, **c**). |
| 12. Evolution during the study & lab inbreeding | **(b), (c)** | Drug-selected lines (`D7`/praziquantel treatments if any) or treated clinical malaria episodes are separated from untreated/wild-type cohorts (**b**). Inbred laboratory strains may understate wild individual duration variance; we compare field/outbred cohorts (`D1`, `D5`, `D6`, `D11`) against laboratory colonies (**c**). |
| 13. Confounding between sites, labs, or host species | **(b)** | Every row in `stage_cv.csv` is computed within a single study $\times$ site/colony $\times$ species $\times$ stage $\times$ treatment cell (**b**). |
