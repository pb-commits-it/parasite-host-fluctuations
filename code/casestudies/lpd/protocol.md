## 1. Question and Scope of Evidence

### 1.1 Primary questions
The project's mathematical theory (`notes/exact-limits.md`, `notes/proofs-v2.md`, `../paper/record/section_ring.html`) shows that how a fluctuating host alters a complex-life-cycle parasite's basic reproduction number $R_0$—and whether time spent in a steady stage ("the parking lot") protects the parasite or harms it—depends on two properties of the host's log-abundance fluctuations:
1. their **variance** $\sigma^2$ (standard deviation $\sigma$), and
2. their **temporal memory** (lag-1 autocorrelation $\phi$, the full autocorrelation function $\rho(\ell)$, and spectral slope $b_{\text{spec}}$).

Several theoretical conclusions reverse sign when host memory is positive and long relative to a steady stage, when host memory is negative or oscillatory, or when $\sigma$ is too large for the second-order small-noise expansion to hold. This study asks:
1. **Empirical distributions:** Across open global compilations of host population time series with at least $N \ge 15$ observations, what are the empirical distributions of detrended log-abundance variance $\sigma^2$, lag-1 autocorrelation $r_1$ (both plug-in and finite-sample bias-corrected $r_{1,\text{bc}}$), full autocorrelation function $\rho(\ell)$, and log-periodogram spectral slope $b_{\text{spec}}$, stratified by **host taxon group** (rodents and other small mammals; birds, with waterfowl and gulls broken out; fish; snails and other freshwater invertebrates; insects that are vectors) and by **sampling interval** (annual versus sub-annual: seasonal, monthly, and weekly/biweekly)?
2. **Theoretical regime mapping:** Across the empirical joint distribution of $(\sigma^2, \text{memory})$ evaluated on the three illustrative two-stage life cycles of `studies/engine-R16/protocol_R16.md`—long adult stage $(1, 24)$, moderate adult stage $(1, 7.5)$, and short adult stage $(1, 2.3)$—what fraction of real host regimes fall where:
   - **(i)** the parking lot works,
   - **(ii)** memory makes a short steady stage harmful, and
   - **(iii)** the second-order small-noise formula is reliable?

### 1.2 Explicit boundary on what abundance compilations can and cannot test
Abundance compilations record host population counts, densities, or indices over time; **none of the open compilations analysed here measures parasite prevalence, infection intensity, or $R_0$ alongside host abundance**. Consequently, this dataset **cannot directly test** the causal prediction that fluctuating host abundance lowers parasite $\ln R_0$ in nature. What it *can* test is whether the host-side environmental premises required by the theory's memory-dependent predictions (specifically, positive temporal memory above the finite-sample linear-detrending white-noise null, versus memoryless white noise or predominantly negative lag-1 autocorrelation) hold in real host populations, and where real hosts sit on the theory's $(\sigma, \phi)$ regime map. It also supplies empirical $(\sigma^2, \rho(\ell), b_{\text{spec}})$ distributions (`results/memory_regimes.json`) to parameterize downstream memory-extension analyses.

### 2.3 Definition of one time series, regular time grid, and eligibility filters
- **GPDD v2:** One `MainID` is one population series. We prefer `PopulationUntransformed` whenever populated and numeric; if only `Population` is present, we inspect `SourceTransform` (`log10`, `ln`/`loge`, `log2` are back-transformed to raw scale before taking natural logs; untransformed counts/densities/indices are used directly; series with undocumented non-invertible standardization taking negative values are excluded). Duplicate `SeriesStep` entries within a `MainID` (if any) are averaged.
- **BioTIME:** Within each eligible `STUDY_ID` and species (`ID_SPECIES` / `GENUS_SPECIES`), sampling effort can vary across time steps if the number of sampled plots/events (`SAMPLE_DESC`) changes. To prevent effort variation from masquerading as host fluctuation (`CASESTUDY_SPEC.md` factor list), the primary abundance index at time step $t$ is the **mean positive/recorded abundance per distinct `SAMPLE_DESC` sampled in that study at time step $t$**:
  $$N_{s,i}(t) = \frac{\sum_{e \in \mathcal{E}_s(t)} A_{s,i,e}}{|\mathcal{E}_s(t)|},$$
  where $\mathcal{E}_s(t)$ is the set of distinct `SAMPLE_DESC` sampling events in study $s$ during time bin $t$ (calendar `YEAR` when the study's modal sampling frequency is $\le 1.5$ time bins/year; `(YEAR, MONTH)` when monthly, i.e., $\ge 6$ distinct months/year; `(YEAR, quarter)` when seasonal, $2\text{–}4$ distinct months/year). Because BioTIME records only positive sightings per sample event, $A_{s,i,e} = 0$ on sampled events $e \in \mathcal{E}_s(t)$ where species $i$ was not observed is automatically accounted for by dividing by $|\mathcal{E}_s(t)|$ (total assemblage sample events in study $s$ at step $t$)! Sensitivity `S6` instead forms series at fixed `(STUDY_ID, PLOT, ID_SPECIES)` without cross-plot pooling.
- **VecDyn:** Within each public dataset, a series is one `(dataset_id, location_key, species, sampling_method, sample_stage)` where `location_key` is `(round(sample_lat_dd, 3), round(sample_long_dd, 3), sample_location)`. Observations (`sample_value`) are aggregated to the series' regular sampling bin (`annual`, `monthly`, or `biweekly` = 14-day / ISO-fortnight bin, or `weekly` = 7-day bin, chosen by the median positive inter-sample interval $\Delta d$ of that dataset-location: $\Delta d \in [4, 10]$ d $\to$ weekly; $[11, 20]$ d $\to$ biweekly; $[21, 45]$ d $\to$ monthly; $[60, 130]$ d $\to$ seasonal; $[300, 400]$ d $\to$ annual), using mean catch per sample event within the bin. Presence/absence (`sample_unit` indicating binary 0/1) is excluded.
- **Regularity and minimum-length rules (primary analysis):**
  1. **Sampling interval class:** `annual` ($\approx 1\text{ yr}^{-1}$), `subannual_seasonal` ($2\text{–}4\text{ yr}^{-1}$), `subannual_monthly` ($\approx 12\text{ yr}^{-1}$), `subannual_biweekly_weekly` ($\approx 26\text{–}52\text{ yr}^{-1}$). Multi-year ($\Delta t > 1.5\text{ yr}$) series are excluded from primary lag-1 comparisons and summarized separately if $\ge 10$ exist.
  2. **Minimum positive observations:** At least **$N \ge 15$** time steps with strictly positive abundance $N(t) > 0$, and non-zero variation ($\operatorname{Var}(\ln N) > 10^{-10}$).
  3. **Zero fraction & grid coverage:** Zero observations ($N(t) = 0$) are treated as missing on the natural-log scale in the primary analysis (matching `R12` and `R14`), provided strictly positive steps make up at least $50\%$ of observed steps and at least **$75\%$ of the integer span** $T_{\text{span}} = t_{\max} - t_{\min} + 1$ (internal missing/zero fraction $\le 25\%$), with at least **$|P_1| \ge 12$** consecutive integer-step pairs $(t, t+1)$ so lag-1 autocorrelation has a genuine one-step physical lag. *(Sensitivity `S1` includes all zeros via the scale-invariant half-minimum replacement $\ln(N(t) + 0.5 N_{\min}^+)$ and relaxes the positive-coverage filter to total observed coverage $\ge 75\%$; Sensitivity `S7` relaxes grid coverage from $75\%$ to $50\%$.)*

## 3. Estimands and Theoretical Predictions

### 3.1 Time-series estimands (per series $i$ on integer step grid $t_1 < \dots < t_n$)
Let $y_k = \ln N(t_k)$ for $k = 1, \dots, n$ ($n \ge 15$).
1. **Linear detrending:** Fit ordinary least squares $y_k = \hat{\alpha} + \hat{\beta} t_k + e_k$.
2. **Variance of log abundance:**
   - Primary detrended log variance: $\hat{\sigma}^2 = \frac{1}{n - 2}\sum_{k=1}^n e_k^2$, with $\hat{\sigma} = \sqrt{\hat{\sigma}^2}$.
   - Companion raw (non-detrended) log variance: $\hat{\sigma}_{\text{raw}}^2 = \frac{1}{n - 1}\sum_{k=1}^n (y_k - \bar{y})^2$.
3. **Autocorrelation function of the detrended log series:**
   With $c_0 = \frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=1}^n e_k^2$ and $P_\ell = \{(a, b) : t_b - t_a = \ell\}$, for $\ell = 1, \dots, \min(10, \lfloor n/3 \rfloor)$ whenever $|P_\ell| \ge 6$:
   $$\hat{\rho}(\ell) = \frac{1}{|P_\ell| c_0} \sum_{(a,b) \in P_\ell} e_a e_b, \qquad \hat{r}_1 = \hat{\rho}(1).$$
4. **Finite-sample detrending null and Kendall–Marriott–Pope bias-corrected lag-1 memory:**
   - Exact white-noise hat-matrix null expectation on $t_1, \dots, t_n$:
     $$\mu_0(\mathbf{t}) = -\frac{n}{(n - 2)|P_1|} \sum_{(a,b) \in P_1} \left[\frac{1}{n} + \frac{(t_a - \bar{t})(t_b - \bar{t})}{\sum_{k=1}^n (t_k - \bar{t})^2}\right], \qquad \Delta r_1 = \hat{r}_1 - \mu_0(\mathbf{t}).$$
   - Bias-corrected AR(1) parameter $\hat{r}_{1,\text{bc}} \in [-0.95, 0.98]$ solving $\frac{\mathbb{E}_{\phi}[\text{num}]}{\mathbb{E}_{\phi}[\text{den}]} = \hat{r}_1$ via the exact $O(n)$ rank-2 projection identity verified in `K0e`.
5. **Spectral colour (slope of the log spectrum):**
   Place $e_k$ on the zero-mean span vector of length $T_{\text{span}} = t_n - t_1 + 1$ (zeros at missing internal slots) and compute the discrete Fourier periodogram $I(f_j) = \frac{1}{n}\left|\sum_{t=0}^{T_{\text{span}}-1} \tilde{e}_t e^{-2\pi i j t / T_{\text{span}}}\right|^2$ over interior 2-df frequencies $f_j = j / T_{\text{span}}$, $j = 1, \dots, \lfloor (T_{\text{span}} - 1)/2 \rfloor$ (excluding $j=0$ and the 1-df Nyquist bin when $T_{\text{span}}$ is even, per `K0d`). The spectral slope $\hat{b}_{\text{spec}}$ is the OLS slope of $\ln I(f_j)$ on $\ln f_j$ (`b_spec < 0` = reddened low-frequency spectrum; `-b_spec` is the positive reddening exponent $\gamma$ in $S(f) \propto f^{-\gamma}$). We also compute the exact finite-$N$ white-noise detrended expectation $\mu_{b,0}(\mathbf{t})$ by 200 Gaussian white-noise draws on $\mathbf{t}$ (`excess_reddening = mu_b0 - b_spec > 0` for redder-than-white series).
6. **Bootstrap sampling errors:**
   - *Within-series SE:* Moving-block bootstrap ($B = 200$ resamples per series, block length $\ell_B = \max(2, \lfloor n^{1/3} \rfloor)$ on the span grid, refitting linear trend on each resample) giving $\text{SE}_{\text{boot}}(\hat{\sigma}^2)$, $\text{SE}_{\text{boot}}(\hat{r}_1)$, and $\text{SE}_{\text{boot}}(\hat{b}_{\text{spec}})$.
   - *Across-series group uncertainty:* Cluster bootstrap ($B_{\text{cl}} = 1,000$ resamples of study clusters—`STUDY_ID` in BioTIME, `DataSourceID` in GPDD, `dataset_id` in VecDyn) for every group median, mean, quantile, and theoretical regime fraction.

### 3.2 Illustrative life cycles and theoretical regime definitions
Following `studies/engine-R16/protocol_R16.md` and `../paper/record/section_ring.html` (`Propositions 2, 4, 18, 20, 21` and `DobsonProofs/Parking.lean`), we evaluate the three illustrative two-stage life cycles:
- **Long adult stage:** exposed fluctuating stay $a = 1$, steady adult stay $b = 24$ ($\mathcal{T}_0 = 25$ coarse steps; fine R16 grid $\Delta t = 1/4$: $a_f = 4, b_f = 96$).
- **Moderate adult stage:** $a = 1, b = 7.5$ ($\mathcal{T}_0 = 8.5$ coarse steps; fine R16 grid: $a_f = 4, b_f = 30$).
- **Short adult stage:** $a = 1, b = 2.3$ ($\mathcal{T}_0 = 3.3$ coarse steps; fine R16 grid: $a_f = 4, b_f = 9.2$).

For each empirical series' $(\sigma, \phi)$—evaluated both at plug-in $(\hat{\sigma}, \operatorname{clip}(\hat{r}_1, -0.95, 0.98))$ and at bias-corrected $(\hat{\sigma}, \hat{r}_{1,\text{bc}})$—we compute:
1. **Second-order threshold loss (`Statement ring-B2` / `Engine.coefficient()['D2']`):**
   $$\Gamma_0(a, b; \sigma, \phi) = \sigma^2 \left[\frac{1 + \phi}{2(1 - \phi)(a + b)} - \frac{R_e(\phi)}{1 - R_e(\phi)}\right], \qquad R_e(\phi) = \frac{\phi^2}{[a - (a-1)\phi][b - (b-1)\phi]},$$
   against the one-stage comparator $\Gamma_0(a; \sigma) = \frac{\sigma^2}{2a}$.
2. **Unexpanded stochastic threshold loss $D(a, b; \sigma, \phi)$ (`lifecycle_engine.py` `Engine.loss`):**
   Evaluated for a mean-one lognormal AR(1) host (`Driver('h0', sigma, phi, 'lognormal')`, primary, where $\Gamma_0$ is the exact quadratic Taylor term of $D$ and the Jensen upper bound is $J = \sigma^2/2$) on a calibrated high-precision Chebyshev/grid surface over $(\sigma, \phi)$ (`steps = 60000, burn = 2000, pairs = 24` per grid node, verified within $2\,\text{SE}$ against 25 direct independent `Engine.loss` evaluations before use), and in sensitivity `S8` for R16's skewed bounded crash host (`Driver('h0', sigma, phi, 'bounded', low=0.05)`).
3. **Exact definitions of the three regime fractions `(i)`, `(ii)`, `(iii)`:**
   Because the literature and project notes use both **threshold-loss comparisons** (`Prop. 18(c)`, `R10b`, `R16`) and **diffusion persistence-index comparisons** (`Result 4` / `Lean P2`, `DobsonProofs/Parking.lean`), both are registered explicitly before seeing data:
   - **(i) "The parking lot works":**
     - *Primary threshold-loss buffering (`i_half_bound`, matching `R10b` / `exact-limits.md` line 49):* Unexpanded loss eliminates at least half of the no-staying Jensen penalty, $0 \le D(1, b; \sigma, \phi) \le \frac{1}{2} J = \frac{\sigma^2}{4}$ (or $D < 0$, a fluctuation gain).
     - *Companion one-stage loss reduction (`i_loss_fine`, on R16's fine memoryless-exposed grid $a_f = 4, b_f = 4b$, $\phi_f = \phi^{1/4}$ for $\phi > 0$ and $\phi_f = 0$ at $\phi = 0$):* $D(4, 4b; \sigma, \phi_f) < D(4; \sigma, \phi_f)$. *(Note: on the coarse grid $a = 1$, $D(1) = \sigma^2/2 = J$ identically, so $D(1, b) < D(1)$ holds trivially for every $b > 1$; `i_half_bound` and `i_loss_fine` are non-trivial.)*
     - *Companion Result 4 / `Lean P2` persistence-index gain (`i_index_P2`):* At illustrative baseline $\ln R_0 \in \{0.35, 1.0\}$ (matching the manuscript's *Borrelia* midpoint $0.35$ and moderate schistosome/malaria benchmark $1.0$, `draft-10.md` §14), $E(1, b) > E(1)$ (`Lean P2`: always holds when $\phi \le 0$ or $\ln R_0 \ge \sigma^2$; when $\phi > 0$ and $0 < \ln R_0 < \sigma^2$, holds iff $b > \frac{\phi}{\phi + 1}\cdot$ wait: at $a=1$, $\frac{a\theta}{a+\theta} = \phi$, so $E(1,b) > E(1) \iff \frac{\ln R_0}{\sigma^2} > \frac{\phi}{\phi + b}$).
   - **(ii) "Memory makes a short steady stage harmful":**
     - *Primary fine-grid threshold-loss reversal (`ii_loss_fine`, `Prop. 18(c)` on R16's memoryless-exposed grid $a_f = 4, b_f = 9.2$):* $\phi > 0$ and adding the short steady stage **raises** loss above having no steady stage: $\Gamma_0(4, 9.2; \sigma, \phi^{1/4}) > \Gamma_0(4; \sigma)$ (analytically $\phi > 0.1919$ at second order; also evaluated on unexpanded $D(4, 9.2) > D(4)$).
     - *Primary Result 4 / `Lean P2` persistence-index reversal (`ii_index_P2`, `exact-limits.md` line 40):* $\phi > 0$ and adding the steady stage $b$ **lowers** the persistence index $E(1, b) < E(1)$ at illustrative $\ln R_0 = 0.35$ (and sensitivity $\ln R_0 \in \{0.20, 1.00\}$, plus at the one-stage stochastic threshold $L = 1/(2a) = 0.5$ where `E(1,b) < E(1)` never occurs for $b > \phi$, i.e. occurs iff $0 < b < \phi$ which is impossible for $b \ge 1$, whereas at $a_f = 4$, $L = 1/8 = 0.125$, the `P8` critical stay is $b_{\text{crit}} = \frac{28\theta_f}{4+\theta_f}$, which exceeds $b_f = 9.2$ whenever $\theta_f > 1.9574 \iff \phi > 0.1919$—the **exact same threshold $\phi > 0.1919$**!).
   - **(iii) "The small-noise formula is reliable":**
     - *Primary (`iii_30pct`, exact R16 criterion from `protocol_R16.md` line 31):* Same sign ($\Gamma_0 \cdot D > 0$, or $|\Gamma_0| < 10^{-4}$ and $|D| < 10^{-4}$) AND relative error $|\Gamma_0 - D| \le 0.30 |D|$.
     - *Strict companion (`iii_10pct`):* Same sign AND $|\Gamma_0 - D| \le 0.10 |D|$.

## 4. Pre-Registered Hypotheses, Decision Criteria, and Power / Identifiability

### 4.1 Registered tests and exact criteria
- **Test `T1` (Positive host memory vs linear-detrending white-noise null):**
  - *Hypothesis:* Real host populations in each of the five target taxon groups have positive intrinsic one-step temporal memory ($\mathbb{E}[\Delta r_1] > 0$, where $\Delta r_1 = \hat{r}_1 - \mu_0(\mathbf{t})$ removes the exact finite-$N$ OLS linear-detrending shift $\mu_0(\mathbf{t})$, corroborated by positive excess spectral reddening $\mathbb{E}[\mu_{b,0}(\mathbf{t}) - \hat{b}_{\text{spec}}] > 0$), rather than white noise ($\phi = 0$) or predominantly negative autocorrelation ($\phi < 0$).
  - *Decision criterion (applied overall and within each taxon $\times$ annual/sub-annual cell having $K \ge 5$ independent studies and $M \ge 25$ series):*
    - **"supports"** positive host memory if the study-clustered 95% confidence interval for mean $\Delta r_1$ lies strictly above $0$ (two-sided $\alpha = 0.05$) **and** median bias-corrected $\hat{r}_{1,\text{bc}} > 0$;
    - **"contradicts"** positive host memory if the study-clustered 95% CI for mean $\Delta r_1$ lies strictly below $0$ (indicating genuine negative / over-compensating lag-1 autocorrelation beyond detrending bias);
    - **"not decided"** if the study-clustered 95% CI for mean $\Delta r_1$ includes $0$ (or if $K < 5$ studies / $M < 25$ series, reported as underpowered).
- **Test `T2` (Substantial departure from the memoryless small-noise regime):**
  - *Hypothesis:* Across real host populations, empirical $(\sigma, \phi)$ regimes are neither universally small-noise nor universally memory-free: specifically, at least $20\%$ of series violate the strict $10\%$ small-noise approximation on the long cycle $(1, 24)$ **or** fall into the positive-memory short-stage penalty zone ($\phi > 0.1919$, `ii_loss_fine`) using bias-corrected memory.
  - *Decision criterion:* **"supports"** if the study-clustered 95% lower bound on at least one of those two fractions exceeds $0.20$; **"contradicts"** if the study-clustered 95% upper bounds on *both* fractions lie below $0.10$; **"not decided"** otherwise.

### 4.2 Pre-analysis simulation power and identifiability (`results/preanalysis_power.txt`)
Before fetching any empirical data, `preanalysis_power.py` simulated $1,500$ synthetic series per cell across $N \in \{15, 20, 30, 50, 100\}$, $\phi \in \{-0.30, 0.00, 0.10, 0.20, 0.40, 0.75\}$, and observation-error variance shares $\eta_{\text{obs}} \in \{0.00, 0.25, 0.50\}$, plus $500$ study-clustered datasets per $(M, \phi, \eta_{\text{obs}})$ cell ($5$ series/study, within-study correlation $0.40$):
1. **Why naive $\hat{r}_1$ would fail catastrophically:** Under pure white noise ($\phi = 0$) with linear detrending, $\mathbb{E}[\hat{r}_1] = -0.147$ at $N = 15$ and $-0.069$ at $N = 30$ (matching the exact hat-matrix formula $-1/7 = -0.1429$ at complete $N=15$). Testing raw $\hat{r}_1 < 0$ falsely declares "negative memory" in **$30.6\%$** of white-noise datasets at $M = 25$, **$91.2\%$** at $M = 100$, and **$100.0\%$** at $M = 300$—and even when true $\phi = +0.10$ with $50\%$ observation error, raw $\hat{r}_1$ falsely declares negative memory $97.0\%$ of the time at $M = 300$.
2. **Type I error and power of the registered test `T1` ($\Delta r_1 = \hat{r}_1 - \mu_0(\mathbf{t})$):**
   - *Type I error at $\phi = 0$:* Two-sided rejection rate is $0.042\text{–}0.052$ at $M = 25$, $0.046\text{–}0.060$ at $M = 100$, and $0.040\text{–}0.066$ at $M = 300$, invariant to observation error $\eta_{\text{obs}} \in [0, 0.50]$.
   - *Power to detect positive memory:*
     - Small stratum ($M = 25, K = 5$ studies): power is $67.8\%$ at $\phi = 0.20$ ($\eta_{\text{obs}}=0$), $99.8\%$ at $\phi = 0.40$ ($\eta_{\text{obs}}=0$), and $68.2\%$ at $\phi = 0.40$ ($\eta_{\text{obs}}=0.50$). **Indistinguishable from no effect:** $0 < |\phi| \le 0.15$ (or $|\phi| \le 0.30$ under $50\%$ observation noise).
     - Medium stratum ($M = 100, K = 20$ studies): power is $86.0\%$ at $\phi = 0.10$ ($\eta_{\text{obs}}=0$) and $93.6\%$ at $\phi = 0.20$ ($\eta_{\text{obs}}=0.50$). **Indistinguishable from no effect:** $|\phi| \le 0.05$ ($\le 0.10$ under $50\%$ observation noise).
     - Large stratum ($M = 300, K = 60$ studies): power is $84.4\%$ at $\phi = 0.05$ ($\eta_{\text{obs}}=0$) and $88.6\%$ at $\phi = 0.10$ ($\eta_{\text{obs}}=0.50$). **Indistinguishable from no effect:** $|\phi| \le 0.03$.
3. **Identifiability limits:**
   - *Observation error:* Uncorrelated log-observation error $\eta_{\text{obs}}$ attenuates $\hat{r}_1$ and $\hat{r}_{1,\text{bc}}$ by $(1 - \eta_{\text{obs}})$ (`E[r1_bc] = +0.390` at $\eta_{\text{obs}}=0$ vs `+0.191` at $\eta_{\text{obs}}=0.50$ for $N=100, \phi=0.40$) and inflates $\hat{\sigma}^2$ by $(1 - \eta_{\text{obs}})^{-1}$ relative to process variance. Thus observed empirical memory is a **lower bound** on true biological memory, and observed $\hat{\sigma}^2$ is an **upper bound** on process variance.
   - *Multi-lag cycles:* For a $3.5$-step AR(2) vole-like quasi-cycle ($\rho(1) = -0.217, \rho(2) = -0.563$), lag-1 $\hat{r}_1 = -0.225$ looks mildly negative while lag-2 $\hat{\rho}(2) = -0.544$ and $\hat{\rho}(3) = +0.309$ carry strong oscillatory memory. Evaluating Proposition 1 directly on the full empirical ACF (`Sensitivity S3`) captures multi-lag returns missed by AR(1).
   - *Finite-$N$ regime bias:* At $N = 20$, plug-in $\hat{r}_1$ underestimates `ii_loss_fine` by $17$ percentage points (`0.447` vs truth `0.617`), whereas bias-corrected $\hat{r}_{1,\text{bc}}$ recovers `0.591` (`0.625` at $N = 40$). Both plug-in and bias-corrected regime fractions will therefore be reported side by side.

3. **NOAA GHCN-Daily station archive and full `<= 250 km` station search:** To match `CASESTUDY_SPEC.md` ("NOAA GHCN-Daily station records") and fix the earlier `[:8]` nearest-station truncation (which checked only the 8 geographically closest stations, often modern post-1950 airports, missing long historical observatories within `250 km`), `data/fetch/lpd-weather.py` fetches `ghcnd-stations.txt`, `ghcnd-inventory.txt`, and individual daily station CSVs (`https://www.ncei.noaa.gov/data/global-historical-climatology-network-daily/access/<ID>.csv`, U.S. Gov Public Domain) supplying daily `TMAX`, `TMIN`, and `PRCP` (`TMEAN = (TMAX + TMIN)/2`, `>= 80%` bin completeness; `>= 240` valid days/year for annual bins), falling back to `GHCN-Monthly v4` only for early 19th-century records predating daily digitization.
4. **Inland GPDD series with `Ocean = "Not applicable"`:** Audit of the `22` series previously unmatched as `no_ocean_grid_within_250km` showed `20` have inland terrestrial/freshwater coordinates (`> 250 km` from ocean, `Ocean = "Not applicable"`): `1` Alpine lake whitefish (`GPDD_2066`, Lake Constance), `1` inland raptor (`GPDD_7047`, Snake River, ID), `4` inland delta muskrats (`GPDD_7088–7091`, Saskatchewan), and `14` sockeye salmon (*Oncorhynchus nerka*) freshwater spawning-ground escapement series in interior British Columbia (`GPDD_1964–2020`). In `r27`, inland freshwater/terrestrial coordinates (`> 250 km` from ocean and `Ocean == "Not applicable"`) are matched to local land climate (`TerraClimate` `<= 15 km` or `GHCN-Daily` `<= 250 km`), and all primary weather tests are reported **both** including and excluding the `14` anadromous *O. nerka* series (whose ocean phase experiences North Pacific SST).

### A3.2 Registered covariates, mechanisms, and three modes of model/analysis entry
1. **Covariates attached per matched observation bin:**
   - Mean air or sea-surface temperature $T_i(t_k)$ (°C), yielding site mean $\bar{T}_i$, detrended SD $\hat{\sigma}_{T,i}$, and bias-corrected lag-1 memory $\hat{r}_{1,\text{bc},T,i}$;
   - Total bin precipitation $P_i(t_k)$ (mm; from Daymet, TerraClimate, and GHCN-Daily), yielding $\bar{P}_i$, detrended SD $\hat{\sigma}_{P,i}$, and memory $\hat{r}_{1,\text{bc},P,i}$;
   - Ambient water vapour pressure $\text{VP}_i(t_k)$ (kPa) and saturation vapour pressure deficit $\text{VPD}_i(t_k) = \max(0, e_s(T_{\max}, T_{\min}) - \text{VP}_i(t_k))$ (kPa, Tetens formula $e_s(T) = 0.61078 \exp(\frac{17.269 T}{T + 237.3})$ averaged across daily $T_{\max}, T_{\min}$) for Daymet-matched North American sub-annual series, capturing desiccation hazard on vectors and free-living stages.
2. **Mode 1 — Adjustment for weather confounding (`R27-W1`, mechanism: shared autocorrelated or unimodal abiotic forcing of host numbers):**
   - **`W1_linear`:** Regress $\ln N_i(t_k)$ on $[1, t_k, T_i(t_k), T_i(t_k - 1)]$ plus $[P_i(t_k), P_i(t_k - 1)]$ whenever precipitation is non-constant and complete on those steps (`df >= 8`).
   - **`W1_quadratic`:** Because ectotherm and host vital rates respond unimodally to temperature (`Kirk et al. 2018`; `Mordecai et al. 2019`), augment `W1_linear` with centered quadratic terms $[T_i(t_k) - \bar{T}_i]^2$ and $[T_i(t_k - 1) - \bar{T}_i]^2$ whenever $n - p \ge 10$, testing whether nonlinear thermal optima generate apparent host memory.
   - **`W1_daymet_vpd`:** On Daymet sub-annual series, augment temperature + precipitation adjustment with $\text{VPD}_i(t_k)$ and $\text{VPD}_i(t_k - 1)$ (`df >= 8`).
   - For every design $X$, residual lag-1 excess correlation $\Delta r_{1,\text{resid}} = \hat{r}_{1,\text{resid}} - \mu_0(\mathbf{t}, Q_X)$ and projected bias-corrected memory $\hat{r}_{1,\text{bc},\text{resid}}$ use the exact orthonormal column space $Q_X$ of $X$, and finite-amplitude losses $D$ on paired unadjusted (`W1_paired`) vs adjusted (`W1_linear`, `W1_quadratic`) host regimes are evaluated **directly with `studies/engine-R16/lifecycle_engine.py`** (`steps = 16,000, burn = 1,200, pairs = 8`, eliminating surface-interpolation ambiguity).
3. **Mode 2 — Driver of stage durations via cited temperature–development relations (`R27-W2`, citing `Kirk et al. 2018`, *PLOS Biology*, DOI `10.1371/journal.pbio.2004608`, and `Mordecai et al. 2019`, *Ecology Letters*, DOI `10.1111/ele.13335`, both read in full with resolved DOIs):**
   - **Boltzmann–Arrhenius (`BA`) relation (`Kirk et al. 2018` Materials & methods; `Mordecai et al. 2019` p. 3):**
     $$a_{\text{BA}}(\bar{T}_i; E_a) = a_{\text{ref}} \exp\!\left[\frac{E_a}{k_B}\left(\frac{1}{\bar{T}_i + 273.15} - \frac{1}{293.15}\right)\right], \qquad k_B = 8.62 \times 10^{-5}\text{ eV}\,\text{K}^{-1},$$
     at canonical metabolic activation energy $E_a = 0.65\text{ eV}$ and sensitivity $E_a \in \{0.50, 0.65, 0.85\}\text{ eV}$ (`Kirk et al. 2018` Table 2: $E_r = 0.669\text{ eV}, E_\mu = 0.801\text{ eV}$; `Mordecai et al. 2019`: $0.6\text{–}0.7\text{ eV}$).
   - **Unimodal modified Sharpe–Schoolfield (`SS`) upper-threshold relation (`Kirk et al. 2018` S1 Fig & Table 2):**
     $$a_{\text{SS}}(\bar{T}_i) = a_{\text{BA}}(\bar{T}_i; 0.65) \frac{1 + \exp\!\left[\frac{E_H}{k_B}\left(\frac{1}{T_{H,K}} - \frac{1}{\bar{T}_i + 273.15}\right)\right]}{1 + \exp\!\left[\frac{E_H}{k_B}\left(\frac{1}{T_{H,K}} - \frac{1}{293.15}\right)\right]}, \qquad E_H = 3.25\text{ eV}, \quad T_{H,K} = 303.15\text{ K } (30\,^\circ\text{C}).$$
   - Anchored on the R16 fine-step scale ($a_{\text{ref}} = 4.0$ steps at $20\,^\circ\text{C}$, paired with sheltered adult stays $b_f = 4b \in \{96, 30, 9.2\}$ so warm tropical sites remain admissible $a(\bar{T}_i) \ge 1$ up to $36.7\,^\circ\text{C}$), and reported both overall and stratified by physical sampling interval (annual vs monthly vs seasonal) and latitude band.
4. **Mode 3 — Factor in `studies/engine-R16/lifecycle_engine.py`'s finite-amplitude sensitivity analysis (`R27-W3`):**
   All `Engine.loss` runs use `steps = 16,000, burn = 1,200, pairs = 8` (validated beforehand against `Engine.coefficient()['D2']` and pure-Python `Engine._products` in checks `R27-K0a`–`R27-K0e`):
   - **`Engine-W3a` (Temperature-driven stage durations in `Engine`):** For every matched series with $\hat{r}_{1,\text{bc},i} \ge 0$ and admissible $a_f(\bar{T}_i) \ge 1$, evaluate `Engine` on `[Stage('exposed', a_f(T_i), 'memoryless'), Stage('long', 4*b, 'memoryless')]` with `Driver('h0', sigma_i, phi_i**0.25, 'lognormal')` under `BA` ($E_a \in \{0.50, 0.65, 0.85\}\text{ eV}$) and `SS` ($T_H = 30\,^\circ\text{C}$) on the short cycle (and `BA` $0.65\text{ eV}$ across all three cycles), comparing against fixed-$T$ `primary_fine` ($a_f = 4.0$) and against the matched one-stage benchmark $D_1(a_f(\bar{T}_i); \hat{\sigma}_i)$.
   - **`Engine-W3b` (Joint correlated fluctuating temperature + host abundance drivers in `Engine`):** On `[Stage('larva_temp', max(1.0, a_BA_coarse(T_i)), 'memoryless'), Stage('exposed_host', 1.0, 'fixed'), Stage('long', b, 'memoryless')]`, couple two dynamic `Driver`s in `Engine`—`Driver('h0', sigma_i, phi_i, 'lognormal')` on host encounter and `Driver('temp', |pi| * sigma_T_i, phi_T_i, 'lognormal')` on thermal larval survival (`pi in {+0.05, -0.05} degC^-1` illustrative thermal completion sensitivity from `notes/mechanism-temperature-incubation.md` §3.2)—with cross-correlation $\rho_{\text{eff}} = \operatorname{sign}(\pi)\operatorname{clip}(\hat{\rho}_{h,T,i}, -0.95\rho_{\max}, +0.95\rho_{\max})$ where $\rho_{\max}(\phi_h, \phi_T) = \frac{\sqrt{(1-\phi_h^2)(1-\phi_T^2)}}{1 - \phi_h\phi_T}$ is the exact causal AR(1) PSD bound. Compare against the host-only ($\sigma_T = 0$) same-duration `Engine` baseline across all matched series!
   - **`Engine-W3c` (Fluctuating thermal clock + waiting mortality via `contact=True` in `Engine`):** On the fine grid (`phi_i >= 0, phi_T_i >= 0`), add a bounded thermal clock stage `Stage('larva_clock', 4.0, 'memoryless', death=0.125, contact=True)` driven by `Driver('temp', 0.08 * sigma_T_i, phi_T_i**0.25, 'bounded', 0.05)` alongside `Stage('exposed_host', 4.0, 'memoryless')` driven by `Driver('h0', sigma_i, phi_i**0.25, 'bounded', 0.05)` and `Stage('long', 4*b, 'memoryless')` across all admissible matched series (`Var < 5.70`), isolating whether dynamic thermal clock + waiting mortality shifts buffering fractions relative to fixed thermal clock (`sigma_T = 0`).

### A3.3 Pre-registered criteria for what would change the study's conclusion
1. **Change in primary host-memory conclusion (`Test T1`):** The study's `T1` conclusion changes if, after linear (`W1_linear`), quadratic unimodal (`W1_quadratic`), or Daymet-VPD (`W1_daymet_vpd`) weather adjustment (or after excluding the `14` anadromous interior-BC *O. nerka* series), the study-clustered 95% CI for mean $\Delta r_{1,\text{resid}}$ includes $0$ (`"not decided"`) or falls strictly below $0$ (`"contradicts"`), or median $\hat{r}_{1,\text{bc},\text{resid}} \le 0$—overall or in any previously `"supports"` taxon $\times$ interval stratum with $K \ge 5, M \ge 25$.
2. **Change in departure-from-small-noise/memoryless conclusion (`Test T2`):** `T2` changes from `"supports"` if, after weather adjustment (`W1_linear` or `W1_quadratic`) or after incorporating empirical temperature in `lifecycle_engine.py` (`Engine-W3a` or `Engine-W3b`), the study-clustered 95% lower bounds on **both** the long-cycle strict 10% small-noise failure fraction and the short-stage memory-harm fraction drop to $\le 0.20$.
3. **Material shift in life-cycle regime fractions:** Even if `T1` and `T2` remain `"supports"`, a weather input is flagged as **quantitatively material to predicted parasite regimes** if it moves any cycle's parking-lot buffering fraction `(i)`, memory-harm fraction `(ii)`, or small-noise accuracy fraction `(iii)` by **more than $10$ percentage points** (or across $50\%$) relative to its matched fixed-temperature host-only `Engine` comparator.
