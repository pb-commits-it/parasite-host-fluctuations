# Spatial mechanism protocol

Written before calculating the outcomes. All parameter values below are illustrative. No network or literature search will be used. Literature statements, if any, will be marked “recalled, not verified”. Only files in this directory will be read or written.

## Model and baselines

The census is immediately after movement. Demography (staying and transfers) occurs first; then one named stage moves. Movement is linear and independent of the environment. Island destinations include the departure patch. Neighbour movement on a circle splits between the two neighbours. On an open line a proposed move beyond either end is lost. Downwind movement has one destination, with loss beyond the last patch on an open line. Transit loss applies only to leavers.

Use stage-major coordinates. The movement block of stage d is K=(1-m)I+m(1-ell)W; other stage blocks are identities. A(t)=M A_demography(t). The noisy transfer is stage 0 to stage 1 (mod n). “Before”, “after”, and “steady” mean dispersal of stage 0, stage 1, and a distinct steady stage, respectively. Spatial Gaussian correlation is rho_s in [0,1], with common temporal AR coefficient phi. One independent host driver per fluctuating transfer unless stated otherwise.

The comparison baseline throughout is (ii): every rate fixed at its arithmetic mean, including movement and edge losses. Here transfer depends linearly on host abundance, so it also equals baseline (i), constant mean host abundance. Mean movement effects will also be compared with the mean matrix at m=0 and named separately.

Define whole-system R0 using the spectral radius of the full-cycle next-generation operator: eliminate within-stage staying using (I-S_j)^(-1), and compose the n successive between-stage operators. Multiplying one transfer everywhere by exp(y) multiplies this R0 by exp(y). For critical mean matrices R0=1, D is the root y of stochastic growth r(y)=0. Otherwise D=log R0+y. Growth at the unshifted arithmetic-mean matrix and threshold loss are separate outputs. Most comparisons calibrate the mean matrix to R0=1 by adjusting that same transfer. Report calibration and the uncalibrated mean movement effect.

## Claims and tests, fixed before outcomes

1. Validate the general C'' expression in the supplied note against the supplied implementation. Derive an island decomposition into the uniform spatial mode and the P-1 transverse modes. Check the derived expression against the full entry-by-entry matrix resolvent to absolute tolerance 2e-10 (relative 2e-8 for very large terms). Check its growth prediction independently with stochastic products and Monte Carlo standard errors. A second-order formula is not a finite-noise identity.
2. H1: search for both positive and negative C'', including rings with identical patches and with illustrative source/sink contrast. At second order locate sign boundaries in m, phi, rho_s by scalar root finding. Require residual below 1e-9. Test examples on both sides with independent full matrix calculation and finite-noise simulation. A finite-noise sign is established computationally only if its 95% confidence interval excludes zero. Report inconclusive cases. Derive any exact finite-state/periodic boundary used and check it with direct Floquet matrix products that do not use the formula.
3. H2: compare the threshold loss for a short versus a long steady stage, at fixed other stays, using mean-critical calibration. Run with and without dispersal. Seek a sign reversal of the benefit, and locate a second-order boundary if present. Explicitly distinguish fixed generation time from lengthening generation time. Test each claimed side by exact matrix products or simulation.
4. H3: compare n=2,3,4 at fixed total generation time, using equal durations at mean criticality; noisy hosts are independent. Also compare one noisy transfer with all transfers noisy, and name which stage moves. Do not interpret host-number effects as a theorem unless a proof is given. Search for a reversal with movement and temporal memory; test both sides of any reported boundary.
5. H4: inspect the supplied large-noise proof, determine whether its path/product argument extends to this spatial matrix and generation R0, and write a proof for any claimed extension. If it does not extend, seek a counterexample. Include synchrony, transit loss, and a reducible open line. A proof is required for an all-noise bound; numerical agreement alone is insufficient.
6. Synchrony: for identical patches and conservative island/circle movement, test equality with one patch at rho_s=1 using the same forcing path. Require growth differences below 1e-10 in deterministic/Floquet checks and below 1e-9 per step in common-path stochastic checks after burn-in. With transit or edge loss, compare against the corresponding changed single-patch mean dynamics when applicable; do not assume equality with the original ring. Test the guessed variance factor rho_s+(1-rho_s)/P at complete mixing with phi=0 against the full C'' expression. Derive the actual fraction of loss removed and state its domain.
7. Directional open line: compute the uncalibrated mean spectral growth and whole-system R0 before and after movement, then separately the variability effect relative to each arithmetic-mean matrix. Compare a circle and an open line. Check analytic edge-loss expressions against eigenvalues/full-cycle operators, tolerance 1e-10.
8. Cover m, P, rho_s, phi, stays, ell, movement stage, topology, and patch quality in a deterministic second-order scan. Use selected finite-noise simulations at sigma=0.3 and 1.0 with reported standard errors. An exhaustive finite-noise phase diagram is not promised.

## Numerical methods and decision rules

Python 3, numpy and scipy only; run `python3 -I mechanism_checks.py`. At most three worker processes. Target final-script wall time below 15 minutes. Use reproducible fixed seeds. Exact deterministic calculations mean numerical eigenvalues, resolvents, root finding, or Floquet products, with stated tolerances; simulated Lyapunov exponents are estimates, never exact values.

Use independent replicates, stationary Gaussian AR starts, burn-in, and normalized positive vector products. Estimate standard errors from replicate variation. Estimate D with common-random-number growth evaluations and root interpolation or bisection, checking the root residual against sampling error. Report run length, replicate count, seed, growth, D, and standard errors. Increase run length or label unresolved if confidence intervals straddle the tested boundary. Keep stochastic root bias and finite horizon limits explicit.

All reported numerical values must be emitted by mechanism_checks.py. Closed forms require a derivation/proof in the note and a separate numerical computation not using that form (full matrix products or stochastic simulation). Numerical resolvent cross-checks alone validate algebra but not finite-noise growth predictions. Every reported boundary must have computed examples on both sides, with its approximation order stated. Threshold surfaces may be given by a measurable matrix/root condition instead of a misleading universal scalar rule.

## Implementation amendment, after the deterministic scan and before simulations

The open directional line has repeated eigenvalues and a defective block triangular matrix. The first run's generic eigensolver failed its eigenvalue check. Keep that output. Compute its spectral radius from strongly connected diagonal blocks, and independently check these against normalized powers. Do not loosen the eigenvalue tolerance.

Use antithetic Gaussian paths: each independent replicate is the average of a path driven by z and one driven by -z. Standard errors are across these independent pairs, not across the correlated individual paths. Initial simulation target: 24 pairs, 60,000 retained steps, 3,000 burn-in, two passes for the threshold. A three-point quadratic interpolation proposes the root; a new direct product at that root checks and corrects its residual using a tangent recursion. Report the correction and its estimated second-order remainder. Reuse forcing for paired comparisons. Selected additional small-noise runs use sigma=0.075. Finite-noise boundaries, if estimated, carry Monte Carlo uncertainty; they are not described as exact closed forms.

Additional exact checks: random-phase periodic forcing, with each patch's arithmetic mean held fixed, permits direct Floquet eigenvalues. Use this only as a separate finite-environment check, not as a substitute for the specified Gaussian AR model. Also check the scalar lognormal model by quadrature. Any special all-noise synchrony or complete-mixing formula will be tested with direct products or scalar quadrature.
