## Part C — the parking lot (claim c)

Two stages. Stage 1 is the long-lived host, with constant survival s1 = 1 − 1/r1. Stage 2 has mean survival ½. Production g is the same in both directions and is set so that the matrix of mean rates has dominant eigenvalue 1.05 for every r1. A hidden Gaussian AR(1) series with autocorrelation 0.9 decides good and bad times: bad when it is negative.

| | Hypothesis | Basis | Kind | Passes if |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| C1 | With survival of stage 2 at 0.95 in good times and 0.05 in bad, long-run growth is 0.0344, 0.0567, 0.0737, 0.0725 for r1 = 1.1, 2, 6, 20 | secondary's script | check | each of my values within 0.001 (several standard errors of either run) |
| C2 | With the same design but the fluctuation moved to production out of stage 2 (1.9 times its mean in good times, 0.1 in bad; survival constant at ½), a long-lived stage "does nothing" | secondary's statement of claim c. Its evidence was a design with total cycle time fixed, where swapping the two stages is an exact symmetry. That is not this comparison | open | "does nothing" stands if the cost at r1 = 20 is within 20% of the cost at r1 = 1.1. My expectation is that it fails: at fixed λ0 a longer stay means less of the growth is owed to production, the elasticity of λ0 to that production step falls from 0.333 to 0.081, and small-noise theory puts the cost near (0.081/0.333)² = 6% of its value at r1 = 1.1 |

Measured: long-run growth with its standard error over 400 replicates of 60,000 steps (1,000 discarded), the variance of log growth per step, and safety = 2 × growth ÷ variance.

## Part D — return to equilibrium (claim d)

Model, written out here and coded from this text: n stages, each left at rate g = n/τ with τ = 2 the total cycle time. P_1' = g R P_n − g P_1 − c P_1², and P_j' = g P_{j−1} − g P_j for j ≥ 2; for n = 1, P' = gRP − gP − cP². R = 15 is the whole-cycle factor and c = 0.005.

| | Hypothesis | Basis | Kind | Passes if |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| D1 | The rate of return to equilibrium (largest real part of the Jacobian's eigenvalues) is −7.000, −0.474, −0.416, −0.391, −0.368, −0.358 for n = 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 8 | secondary's script, which writes the Jacobian down directly | check | my values within 0.002, from a Jacobian obtained by finite differences at an equilibrium found by integrating the equations; and the decay rate fitted to a simulated trajectory within 2% |
| D2 | Almost all of the weakening happens between one and two stages, and comes from adding a stage with no density dependence, not from the number of stages | For large R the characteristic equation (x + g(2R−1))(x + g)^(n−1) = gⁿR has leading root near g(2^(−1/(n−1)) − 1) for n ≥ 2, which runs only from −0.50 to −0.35 as n goes from 2 to infinity | open | the return rate at n = 8 is at least 70% of that at n = 2 |
| D3 | The slowest mode is not oscillatory at any n tried: the complex eigenvalues decay faster than the real leading one | same characteristic equation | open | the eigenvalue with the largest real part is real for every n |
