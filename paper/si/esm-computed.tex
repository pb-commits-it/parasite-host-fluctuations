% ESM: Computed studies
\section{Computed studies and whole-number populations}\label{num-esm-computed}

This section records the protocols, equations, complete summary tables, second-party reproductions, and post-review corrections for the computed studies summarized in Section~\ref{num-computed}. None of these calculations fits a model to field parasite trajectories, and parameter combinations assembled from published literature represent illustrative inputs drawn from distinct populations rather than measurements on a single population.

\subsection{Losses without the small-noise expansion at synthetic and illustrative real-system inputs (R10)}\label{num-esm-r10}

To evaluate the exact discrete-time threshold loss $D = \ln R_0 - m\ln R_0^*$ without truncating at $O(\sigma^2)$, run R10 simulated discrete rings at threshold driven by stationary mean-one lognormal $\mathrm{AR}(1)$ multipliers (200 populations followed for $200{,}000$ steps each after burn-in, using common random numbers across trial shifts on one designated transfer to locate zero long-run growth; computed once). Across 161 parameter sets, validation checks passed before reading the open grids: in 18 of 18 single-stage cases ($s \in \{0.5, 0.9, 0.96\}$, $\sigma \in \{0.6, 1.0, 1.4\}$, with and without memory), simulated $D$ agreed with numerical quadrature within $0.0006$ (for example, $0.0698 \pm 0.0004$ vs.\ $0.0692$ at $s = 0.9, \sigma = 1.0$ with memory); in two three-stage rings with no staying at $\sigma = 1$, simulated $D$ was $1.5003 \pm 0.0005$ and $1.5004 \pm 0.0008$ against the exact value $\sum_{k=1}^3 \sigma_k^2/2 = 1.5$; and at $\sigma = 0.2$, $D/\Gamma_0$ was $0.976$ and $1.006$. In 161 of 161 cases, simulated $D$ satisfied $D \le \sum_k \sigma_k^2/2$ within sampling uncertainty, and $D \ge 0$ held in every memoryless-host case.

On synthetic two- and three-stage rings across $\sigma \in \{0.2, 0.6, 1.0, 1.4, 2.0\}$ and $\phi \in \{0, 0.4, 0.75\}$ (Table~\ref{num-esm-tab-r10-h3}), the second-order value $\Gamma_0$ never exceeded $D$ on this grid (though $\Gamma_0 \le D$ is not a general theorem across all host laws or parameters). The ratio $D/\Gamma_0$ increased with $\sigma$ and was largest for a long steady stay paired with an uncorrelated host ($2.14$ at $\sigma = 1.4$ and $3.37$ at $\sigma = 2.0$ for stays $(1, 24)$ at $\phi = 0$), while remaining within $2\%$ of unity up to $\sigma = 2.0$ for short cycles at $\phi = 0.75$. Similarly, when a steady stage of mean stay $b \in \{2, 5, 19, 50, 200\}$ was added to a fluctuating stage of stay $10$ with strong host memory $\phi = 0.9$ (Table~\ref{num-esm-tab-r10-h5}), $D$ rose above the one-stage value at short steady stays for every $\sigma \in \{0.3, 0.6, 1.0, 1.4\}$ ($3.7\times$ at $b = 5, \sigma = 0.3$ and $2.4\times$ at $b = 5, \sigma = 1.4$) and fell below the one-stage loss between $b = 50$ and $b = 200$ on the sampled grid.

Table~\ref{num-esm-tab-r10-sys} gives the nine illustrative parameter sets assembled from published literature for named host--parasite systems, setting the discrete step $\Delta t$ equal to the shortest stage stay so $\tau_{\min} = 1$ and modeling every stage stay as geometric on that grid \citep{paterson1998,lewellen1998,milner1999,kao2000,haydon2002,ishikawa2003,elias2004,lambin2006,smith2007,new2009,jacob2011,ndiath2012,atkinson2013,dunn2013,colley2014,martinezpadilla2014,anderson2015,ototo2015,alvarezrojas2018,gurarie2018,rabone2019,anderson2021,tufts2021,whittaker2022}. Eight of the nine rows vary a single host, whereas the fox-and-vole row for \emph{Echinococcus multilocularis} varies two independent hosts ($\sigma = 1.174, \phi = 0.75$ on egg-to-vole transfer and $\sigma = 0.18, \phi = 0.50$ on vole-to-fox transfer; for the one-host \emph{E.~multilocularis} row, placing the sole fluctuating host on any of the three transfers gave $0.4952, 0.4955,$ and $0.4944$, making the eleven-run check set reproduced in R10b). Across the nine rows, $D$ exceeded the discrete second-order value $\Gamma(\phi)$ by $0\%$ to $63\%$ (largest in \emph{Schistosoma mansoni}, $0.1068 \pm 0.0004$ vs.\ $0.0656$), while discrete $\Gamma(\phi)$ itself was $1.44\times$ to $2.02\times$ the continuous-time Ornstein--Uhlenbeck value $\Gamma_{\mathrm{CT}}$ because setting $\Delta t$ to the shortest stay imposes $\tau_{\min} = 1$ ($s_{\min} = 0$). Three source-audit notes apply to Table~\ref{num-esm-tab-r10-sys}: Senegal biting-rate swings come from \citet{ndiath2012}; both mouse log-abundance SDs ($0.668$ converted from $\log_{10}$ midpoints and $1.19$ in \citealp{elias2004}) are on the natural-log scale; and squaring the two-host next-generation eigenvalue once gives whole-cycle $R_0 \in [1.19, 1.74]$ ($\ln R_0 \in [0.17, 0.55]$; whole-cycle interquartile range [IQR] $[0.62, 4.41]$) in \citet{dunn2013} and \citet{tufts2021} rather than the doubly squared upper figure $3.0$ printed in an early table. A screened three-host trematode candidate (\emph{Diplostomum spathaceum}) lacked published whole-cycle $R_0$, field metacercarial mortality, and co-located host series and was omitted rather than guessed.

\subsection{Stay-shape recomputation of the illustrative parasite table (R10b) and measured stage-duration spreads}\label{num-esm-r10b}

In run R10, every row except \emph{E.~multilocularis} assigns the fluctuating stage a duration of one coarse step ($\tau_{\min} = 1$, $s_{\min} = 0$), so the long steady stage is the sole source of return-time dispersion on that grid. Run R10b (136 cases, computed once and audited in a subsequent formal review) replaced the long steady stage in the six complex-cycle rows of Table~\ref{num-esm-tab-r10-sys} with serial chains of $m$ equal geometric sub-stages of the same mean stay, both on R10's coarse step (Part~A) and on an $8\times$ finer step ($11\times$ for \emph{B.~burgdorferi}, matching $\sigma$ and whole-coarse-step autocorrelation via fine-step memory $\phi^{1/r}$; Part~B), alongside a second regular family combining a fixed integer delay with one geometric sub-stage. Pre-run checks reproduced all 11 R10 cases within $1.9$ combined SE, agreed with a second independent simulator on eight of eight test sets within $1.5$ combined SE, matched the primitive second-order transform formula with $R = \E[\phi^L]$ to the matrix resolvent within $4 \times 10^{-13}$ across 112 of 112 simulated primitive shapes, and maintained consistency between run halves within $2.9$ SE across 112 of 112 cases.

Table~\ref{num-esm-tab-r10b-a} reports $D$ as a fraction of the single-host cap $\sigma^2/2$ across Part~A and Part~B (where Part~B holds the fluctuating stage at its fixed block length except in the \emph{E.~multilocularis} egg row, so Part~B $m = 1$ entries are geometric-long-stay rather than all-geometric comparisons). Under the registered majority-protection rule ($D < \sigma^2/4$ by more than $3\,\mathrm{SE}$), both schistosome rows survived at $m = 2, 4,$ and $16$ on both grids ($0.100$--$0.157$ of $\sigma^2/2$ in Part~A; $0.085$--$0.116$ in Part~B; $0.119, 0.134, 0.161$ at $m = 20, 22, 23$ with $\mathrm{CV} = 0.09, 0.06, 0.04$ in \emph{S.~haematobium}, and $0.187, 0.229, 0.285$ in \emph{S.~mansoni}) and failed among the tested shapes at the all-fixed endpoint, where every stage has deterministic integer duration and ergodicity of the Gaussian $\mathrm{AR}(1)$ host ($|\phi| < 1$) under the full-cycle shift gives $D = -\E[\ln h] = \sigma^2/2$ by cohort tracking (at that non-primitive endpoint the formal renewal expression gives $0.0161$ against exact $0.1735$ in \emph{S.~haematobium} and does not apply). In contrast, \emph{P.~falciparum} rose steadily with regularity ($0.336, 0.501, 0.607, 0.704$ of $\sigma^2/2$ at $m = 1, 2, 4, 7$ in Part~A; $0.264, 0.434, 0.538, 0.631$ at $m = 1, 2, 4, 16$ in Part~B), failing majority protection by $m = 4$. Protection in \emph{B.~burgdorferi} was marginal or absent from the outset ($0.482$ and $0.572$ at $m = 1$ in Part~A, failing at $m = 2$; $0.345$ and $0.433$ at $m = 1$ in Part~B), and \emph{E.~multilocularis} exceeded $\sigma^2/4$ even at $m = 1$ ($0.719$ in Part~A, $0.585$ in Part~B, and $0.821$ or $0.739$ when only the vole stage was fixed while egg timing remained geometric).

Table~\ref{num-esm-tab-r10b-b} compares spread in the short fluctuating stage against spread in the long steady stage on the finer step and lists the family-specific brackets for $\mathrm{CV}_{1/2}$, the long-stage CV at which half of the geometric protection $\sigma^2/2 - D_{m=1}$ is lost. While the long stage is geometric, replacing a fixed fluctuating stage with a four-substage chain alters $D$ by at most $7\%$ and making it geometric reduces $D$ by $10\%$ to $24\%$ ($D/(\sigma^2/2) = 0.051, 0.070, 0.230, 0.261, 0.332, 0.585$), whereas fixing the long stage multiplies $D$ by $1.5\times$ to $16.8\times$ when the fluctuating stage is fixed and by $1.3\times$ to $3.6\times$ when the fluctuating stage is geometric. When the long stage is fixed, geometric spread in the snail stage lowers asymptotic $D/(\sigma^2/2)$ from $1.000$ to $0.154$ and $0.255$ in the schistosomes, but cohort relaxation diagnostics there reach $37$ generations ($154\ \mathrm{yr}$, or $254$ generations and $\sim 1{,}060\ \mathrm{yr}$ for a four-substage snail chain, and $30$ generations at $\mathrm{CV} = 0.043$ vs.\ $< 2.6$ generations for $m \le 16$), flagging an uncomputed finite-generation question. As clarified in the appended review note to R10b, within primitive second-order models $D_2$ depends on both $\mathcal{T} = \E[L]$ and $R = \E[\phi^L]$ (mean-preserving spread lowers $D_2$ for $0 < \phi < 1$, whereas $\mathcal{T}$ governs $\phi = 0$), and the $\mathrm{CV}_{1/2}$ brackets in Table~\ref{num-esm-tab-r10b-b} ($[0, 0.043]$ coarse chain, $[0, 0.102]$ fine chain, and $[0, 0.039]$ fine fixed-plus-geometric for schistosomes; interpolated $0.19$--$0.24$ for \emph{P.~falciparum}) are family- and step-specific summaries distinct from $D < \sigma^2/4$. In particular, $0.04$ is the upper end of two tested schistosome brackets across a sparsely sampled steep interval rather than a point estimate or a distribution-free biological cutoff.

\subsubsection{Measured stage-duration spreads and failure of CV-sufficiency (\texttt{devtimes})}\label{num-esm-devtimes}

Because none of the repository source cards behind Table~\ref{num-esm-tab-r10-sys} contained a measured individual stage-duration distribution, a registered compilation (\texttt{devtimes}) extracted single-species, single-stage, single-treatment cohorts from 16 open repositories and compilations \citep{gleichsner2016,shapiro2017,leclech2019,civitello2020,fracasso2022,nguyen2021,stopard2021,brunner2022,taskinen2022,aleuy2023,andrade2024}, separating individual variation from mean-only tables, SE-without-$n$ entries, container-mean SDs, $>50\%$ right-censoring, and cross-temperature plasticity (560 logged exclusion entries; four of 16 assigned deposits yielded zero qualifying rows). Twelve of 16 deposits yielded 485 cohorts (202 uncensored individual cohorts, 35 right-censored Weibull maximum-likelihood cohorts, 46 cross-sectional Gamma/Weibull transition curves, 196 reported-SD rows, and six verified-$n$ SE rows), of which 383 of 485 formed the strict known-$n \ge 10$ primary subset (median $\mathrm{CV}_{\mathrm{indiv}} = 0.2215$, IQR $[0.1228, 0.3540]$, range $[0.0110, 1.3069]$; across all 485 rows, median $0.1867$, IQR $[0.1069, 0.3235]$). Temperature strongly accelerated mean ectotherm pace ($35$ of $39$ multi-temperature series had negative Spearman correlation, median $\rho = -0.943$), yet $442$ of $485$ cohorts ($340$ of $383$ strict cohorts) came from constant-temperature regimes with nearly identical median $\mathrm{CV}_{\mathrm{indiv}}$ ($0.1824$ all, $0.2170$ strict), and temperature explained $0.32\%$ of variance in $\mathrm{CV}_{\mathrm{indiv}}$ across $479$ of $485$ numeric-temperature rows.

Three registered tests link these empirical spreads to R10b:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \emph{Developmental clocks versus survival and clearance stays (\texttt{H\_dev1}: supports).} Across the 383 strict primary cohorts (2,000 bootstrap resamples), physiological development, moulting, and incubation cohorts ($276$ of $383$) had median $\mathrm{CV}_{\mathrm{indiv}} = 0.2035$ (IQR $[0.1182, 0.3104]$, $95\%$ bootstrap confidence interval [CI] $[0.1655, 0.2254] < 0.50$), whereas adult survival, off-host survival, and vertebrate clearance cohorts ($107$ of $383$) had median $\mathrm{CV}_{\mathrm{indiv}} = 0.2935$ (IQR $[0.1693, 0.4564]$, $95\%$ CI $[0.2421, 0.3413]$), with median difference $+0.0900$ ($95\%$ CI $[+0.0327, +0.1545] > 0$). Separation was wider in the 40 field or outbred cohorts ($11$ developmental vs.\ $29$ survival, medians $0.1453$ vs.\ $0.4676$, difference $+0.3223$) than in the 343 benign laboratory cohorts ($0.2098$ vs.\ $0.2585$).
\item \emph{Placement of the illustrative parasite rows (\texttt{H\_sys}: overall not decided as a uniform rule; Table~\ref{num-esm-tab-devtimes}).} One of six main rows was registered as robust (\emph{P.~falciparum}: direct longitudinal genotyping of $1{,}184$ clonal infections in three cohorts from Kalifabougou, Mali \citep{andrade2024} gave near-exponential to hyper-exponential blood-stage $\mathrm{CV}_{\mathrm{indiv}} = 0.7333, 1.1872, 1.3069$, keeping $D = 0.1400$--$0.4458 < \sigma^2/4 - 3\,\mathrm{SE}$ across $0$--$3$ genotyping skip rules, whereas \emph{P.~falciparum}'s mosquito incubation stage in 13 cohorts had tight $\mathrm{CV}_{\mathrm{indiv}} = 0.0388$--$0.1950$ where regularity shifted $D$ by $< 0.0011$). One of six main rows was fragile (northern-edge \emph{B.~burgdorferi}, failing across the 10th--90th percentiles of 35 direct \emph{I.~scapularis} cohorts \citep{brunner2022}, $D/(\sigma^2/2) = 0.543$--$0.695$, as did auxiliary one-host nematodes \emph{T.~tenuis} and \emph{T.~circumcincta} when \texttt{surv\_like} median $\mathrm{CV} = 0.2935$ replaced exponential adult lifespans, raising $D/(\sigma^2/2)$ from $0.154$ and $0.417$ to $0.623$ and $0.809$). \subsection{Whole-number extinction without trajectory censoring (R13b)}\label{num-esm-r13b}

\subsection{Packets, shared host mortality, contact outages, and single-gap retention (R18)}\label{num-esm-r18}

Run R18 modeled whole-number fluctuating-stage parasites $F$ (mean stay $10$ steps, exit probability $0.1$) and steady-stage parasites $S = \sum_{b \ge 1} b C_b$ distributed across $C_b$ occupied steady hosts of positive integer burden $b \ge 1$ (empty hosts removed immediately). For steady mean stay $L \in \{10, 40, 160\}$ and $s = 1 - 1/L$, each occupied host survives a step with probability $s_H = s^a$ and each parasite inside a surviving host survives independently with probability $s_I = s^{1-a}$ ($a \in \{0, 0.5, 1\}$ denoting the host-mortality share of $-\ln s$, so $s_H s_I = s$ and parasite mean stay equals $L$ for every $a$). Before mortality, steady parasites shed independent $\operatorname{Poisson}(g_S S)$ transmission stages into $F$ (amended before the production run to avoid coupling shedding variance to $a$; historical R13b reproduction used R13b's terminal-offspring sampler separately, agreeing within $0.53$ combined SE on three of three stays). Exiting $F$ individuals generate a Poisson count of founding packets of mean $g_F H_t F / \E[K]$, where each packet has fixed size $K \in \{1, 10, 100\}$ (or an equal mixture of $1$ and $19$ with mean $10$) and founds a new steady host without merging. Conditional on $H_t$, every $(a, K, b_0)$ law has the same $2 \times 2$ parasite mean matrix
\begin{equation}\label{num-esm-eq-r18-mean}
\mathbf{A}(H_t) = \begin{pmatrix} 0.9 & g_S \\ g_F H_t & 1 - 1/L \end{pmatrix}, \qquad g_F = \frac{1}{10} + r, \quad g_S = \frac{1}{L} + r,
\end{equation}
while one-step steady survival variance for a host of burden $b$ is $\operatorname{Var}(I Y) = s_H b s_I(1 - s_I) + s_H(1 - s_H)(b s_I)^2$, increasing with $a$ via pair covariances $b(b - 1)(s^{2-a} - s^2)$ (and at $a = 0$, total-population trajectories are distributionally invariant to initial host labels by induction). Setting $g_F$ and $g_S$ as in~\eqref{num-esm-eq-r18-mean} gives $\mathbf{A}(1)$ Perron root $1 + r$ and left eigenvector $(1, 1)$, so every start with $F_0 = 0$ and $S_0 = 10{,}000$ ($10{,}000$ singleton hosts at $b_0 = 1$, or $100$ hosts of burden $b_0 = 100$) has mean-matrix reproductive value $10{,}000$. Each $(L, \text{environment})$ pair was calibrated so the continuous random-matrix Lyapunov exponent $\lambda_{\mathrm{rand}}$ satisfied $(L + 10)\lambda_{\mathrm{rand}} = 0.005$ per nominal generation, reporting $D = (L + 10)[\ln(1 + r) - \lambda_{\mathrm{rand}}]$ and $R_0 = (1 + 10r)(1 + Lr)$ (Table~\ref{num-esm-tab-r18-cal}). Five environments with $\E[H_t] = 1$ were used: R13b's lognormal $\mathrm{AR}(1)$ host ($\mathrm{A}$, $\sigma = 0.15, \phi = 0.9, \operatorname{Var}(H) = 0.0228$) and symmetric two-state Markov outage hosts flipping between $H = 0$ and $H = 2$ with mean state duration $G \in \{5, 20, 60, 200\}$ ($\mathrm{B5}$, $\mathrm{B20}$, $\mathrm{B60}$, $\mathrm{B200}$, $\operatorname{Var}(H) = 1$). Because there is no grouped no-steady-stage comparator, R18 tests longer against shorter steady stays, and changing $L \in \{10, 40, 160\}$ on the common $1{,}700$-step calendar clock simultaneously changes nominal generation count ($85, 34, 10$), cumulative continuous drift ($+0.425, +0.170, +0.050$, making $L = 160$ conservative with respect to positive drift), stage fecundity split $(1 + 10r)$ versus $(1 + Lr)$, the gap between $(L + 10)\ln(1 + r)$ and $\ln R_0$, initial-versus-stable stage share, and incidence of the $10^9$ moment-matched Gaussian switch ($22{,}385$ of $362{,}000$ trajectories in the 192-cell run, up to $531$ of $1{,}000$ at $L = 10$ vs.\ $0$ in all primary $L = 160$ cells; no trajectory was censored).

Validation passed across three constant-environment generating-function checks ($10{,}000$ replicates, within $1.99\,\mathrm{SE}$), 162 two-coordinate mean checks ($2{,}000$ replicates each, worst $2.89\,\mathrm{SE}$), 40 cohort gap checks ($20{,}000$ cohorts each, worst $1.92\,\mathrm{SE}$), and three forced-$10^3$ Gaussian outage return checks ($1{,}000$ replicates each, within $1.02\,\mathrm{SE}$). An independent second-party implementation from scratch checked all three exact-equivalent transition branches (\texttt{direct}, \texttt{pooled}, \texttt{dense}), verified an exact 11-type mixed-mortality generating function at $a = 0.5, K = 10$ ($0.6510$ vs.\ $0.65241$, $0.21\,\mathrm{SE}$), recomputed 18 of 18 continuous calibrations ($0.03$--$1.77$ combined SE) and 21 of 21 factorial and supplemental cells ($n = 2{,}000$--$3{,}000$, all within $0.00$--$2.42$ combined SE), and recounted every contrast across the 195 completed cells ($365{,}000$ trajectories).

\begin{proposition}[Single-gap cohort retention and four-phase lifetimes]\label{num-esm-prop-gap}
Consider $M \ge 1$ initially occupied steady hosts $j = 1, \dots, M$ carrying integer parasite burdens $b_j \ge 1$ across a contact outage of fixed length $G > 0$ with zero new host infections during the gap. Assume distinct hosts have independent lifetimes with gap-survival probability $h(G) \in [0, 1]$, and individual parasites have independent within-host lifetimes, independent of host lifetimes, with gap-survival probability $p(G) \in [0, 1]$. Then the probability that at least one initially stored steady parasite remains in a living steady host at the end of the gap is
\begin{equation}\label{num-esm-eq-gap}
P_{\mathrm{retain}}(G) = 1 - \prod_{j=1}^{M}\Bigl\{1 - h(G)\bigl[1 - (1 - p(G))^{b_j}\bigr]\Bigr\},
\end{equation}
which satisfies $1 - \mathrm{e}^{-\Lambda} \le P_{\mathrm{retain}}(G) \le \min(1, \Lambda)$ for $\Lambda = \sum_{j=1}^M h(G)[1 - (1 - p(G))^{b_j}]$ and increases monotonically when both $h(G)$ and $p(G)$ increase at fixed $(b_1, \dots, b_M)$. For continuous four-phase Erlang host lifetimes of mean $\tau > 0$ ($x = 4G/\tau$), survival of a freshly infected host and of a stationary-age host sampled under constant recruitment are
\begin{equation}\label{num-esm-eq-erlang4}
h_4(G) = \mathrm{e}^{-x}\sum_{j=0}^{3}\frac{x^j}{j!}, \qquad h_{4,\mathrm{res}}(G) = \frac{1}{4}\sum_{r=1}^{4}\mathrm{e}^{-x}\sum_{j=0}^{r-1}\frac{x^j}{j!}.
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Conditional on host $j$ surviving the gap (probability $h(G)$), all $b_j \ge 1$ independent within-host parasite clocks fail with probability $(1 - p(G))^{b_j}$ (taking $0^{b_j} = 0$ at $p(G) = 1$), so host $j$ retains at least one parasite with probability $u_j = h(G)[1 - (1 - p(G))^{b_j}]$. Because hosts are independent and no new hosts are infected during the gap, all $M$ hosts lose their burdens with probability $\prod_{j=1}^M (1 - u_j)$, giving~\eqref{num-esm-eq-gap}. Using $1 - u_j \le \mathrm{e}^{-u_j}$ yields $P_{\mathrm{retain}}(G) \ge 1 - \mathrm{e}^{-\Lambda}$, while Boole's inequality yields $P_{\mathrm{retain}}(G) \le \min(1, \Lambda)$; increasing $h(G)$ and $p(G)$ increases each $u_j$ and thus $P_{\mathrm{retain}}(G)$. Under four independent exponential phases each of rate $4/\tau$, phase completions form a rate-$4/\tau$ Poisson process, so a fresh host survives time $G$ if and only if at most three completions occur, giving $h_4(G)$ in~\eqref{num-esm-eq-erlang4}. Under constant recruitment, stationary occupancy across four equal-rate exponential phases is uniform ($1/4$ each) and memorylessness within the active phase leaves $r \in \{1, 2, 3, 4\}$ remaining phases with probability $1/4$ each, giving $h_{4,\mathrm{res}}(G)$ (the discrete four-phase counterpart with per-step advance $q_4 = 4/L$ has exact gap survival $\mathbb{P}[\operatorname{Binomial}(G, q_4) \le 3]$).
\end{proof}

Proposition~\ref{num-esm-prop-gap} measures survival of initially stored steady parasites and is distinct from full-cycle survival because $F$ offspring can bridge brief steady-host zeros ($2{,}568$ surviving trajectories in the 192-cell run hit zero occupied steady hosts transiently). Under pure shared host death ($a = 1, p(G) = 1$), $P_{\mathrm{retain}}(G) = 1 - (1 - h(G))^M$ depends on occupied host count $M$ rather than total burden $\sum_j b_j$. Solving $1 - (1 - h(G))^{100} = 0.05$ requires $h(G) = 5.1280 \times 10^{-4}$, giving $5\%$ retention at $G/\tau = 7.576$ ($7.58$) mean lifetimes for fresh exponential hosts versus $3.476$ ($3.48$) for fresh four-phase hosts and $3.109$ for stationary-age four-phase hosts (Table~\ref{num-esm-tab-r18-gap}; in \texttt{devtimes}, substituting measured ectotherm survival regularity $\mathrm{CV} = 0.214$--$0.294$ shrinks this $5\%$ boundary to $1.85$--$2.25$ mean lifetimes, whereas overdispersed human \emph{P.~falciparum} stays at $\mathrm{CV} = 1.187$ extend it to $9.49$ mean lifetimes). Because $h_4(G) > \mathrm{e}^{-G/\tau}$ for $G/\tau < 1.178$ ($h > 0.308$) and $h_4(G) < \mathrm{e}^{-G/\tau}$ for $G/\tau > 1.178$, four-phase lifetimes shorten upper-tail retention boundaries without being worse at every gap length.

\subsection{Five mechanisms combined in one discrete-time engine (R16)}\label{num-esm-r16}

Run R16 crossed five binary switches ($\mathrm{ESAPM}$) across five illustrative $(\text{cycle}, \sigma)$ backgrounds and two discrete step sizes ($\Delta t = 1$ coarse, fixed one-step exposed stage; $\Delta t = 1/4$ fine, memoryless exposed stage of mean four fine steps), requesting $5 \times 2 \times 32 = 320$ cells:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $\mathrm{E}$ (\emph{contact-limited leaving}): scarcity delays exit from the exposed stage with half dying while waiting ($\mu = c$), requiring memoryless timing, mean $> 1$, and positive staying at every joint host state;
\item $\mathrm{S}$ (\emph{stay shape}): replacing the memoryless long steady stage with a serial chain of four equal geometric sub-stages of the same mean stay;
\item $\mathrm{A}$ (\emph{alternative hosts}): splitting the fluctuating transfer across two equal-share ($1/2$ each), independent, identically distributed hosts;
\item $\mathrm{P}$ (\emph{spatial patches}): four asynchronous island patches with post-demography movement fraction $m = 0.15$ per coarse step in the mobile stage ($m_{\mathrm{fine}} = 1 - 0.85^{1/4}$, satisfying $\mathbf{K}(m_{\mathrm{fine}})^4 = \mathbf{K}(0.15)$ exactly);
\item $\mathrm{M}$ (\emph{storage mortality}): $1\%$ per-coarse-step mortality during the long steady stage ($1 - 0.99^{1/4}$ per fine step; direct deterministic death cost is reported separately and compensated before measuring $D$).
\end{enumerate}
The five illustrative backgrounds (coarse long-stage mean stay, $\sigma$, coarse host memory $\phi$) were: cycle $0$ ($24$, $\sigma = 0.6$, $\phi = 0.4$), cycle $1$ ($24$, $\sigma = 1.4$, $\phi = 0.4$), cycle $2$ ($7.5$, $\sigma = 1.4$, $\phi = 0.75$), cycle $3$ ($2.3$, $\sigma = 0.7$, $\phi = 0.3$), and cycle $4$ ($2.3$, $\sigma = 1.2$, $\phi = 0.3$). Every cell (including $\mathrm{E} = 0$) used a bounded two-state Markov host with fixed raw lower level $h_{\mathrm{low}} = 0.05$ and variance $\operatorname{Var}(h) = \mathrm{e}^{\sigma^2} - 1$ ($8$ antithetic pairs $\times 12{,}000$ coarse or $48{,}000$ fine steps for $D$; $16$ environments $\times 96$ reference lineages for $V$; $128$ backward-generating-function Poisson branching trials in each of the 32 moderate-cycle cells tilted to $\widehat{D} + 0.02$). As proved in Appendix~\ref{app:mech-r16}, 192 of 320 requested cells are admissible at these two step sizes and 128 of 320 are inadmissible (80 coarse $\mathrm{E} = 1$ cells where a one-step wait forces zero variance, 16 coarse $\mathrm{E} = 0, \mathrm{S} = 1$ short-cycle cells where $2.3 < 4$, and 32 fine $\mathrm{E} = 1$ cells at $\sigma = 1.4$ where $\mathrm{e}^{1.96} - 1 = 6.0993$ exceeds the strict staying bound $5.70$).

An independent second-party implementation from scratch audited the engine against all single-mechanism closed forms, verified 320 of 320 admissibility flags and every contrast across the 192 computed rows, and independently resimulated 17 sample cells spanning all five cycles, both grids, and all five switches at $3\times$ the antithetic pairs ($24$) and $2\times$ the horizon ($24{,}000$ coarse / $96{,}000$ fine steps; Table~\ref{num-esm-tab-r16-check}). Seventeen of 17 cells agreed on $J$, $J_2$, $D_2$ (lag sum and resolvent), and death cost to $< 10^{-10}$ relative error and on simulated $D$ within $0.09$--$2.85$ combined SE. (The audit also noted that the engine's unused forward \texttt{shared\_groups} option grouped outgoing leavers while keeping in-stage staying binomial across individuals; R16 used only the exact Poisson backward generating-function recursion.)
